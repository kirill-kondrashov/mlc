                                                                               1. Introduction
                                           1.1. Brief history. The MLC Conjecture on the local connectivity of the Man-
                                           delbrot set M, put forward by Douady and Hubbard in the mid 1980s [DH1], is
                                           the central problem of contemporary Holomorphic Dynamics. It would imply a
                                           precise topological model for M and the Fatou Conjecture on the density of hy-
                                           perbolic maps in M, and it is intimately related to the Mostow-Thurston Rigidity
                                           phenomenon in 3D Hyperbolic Geometry.
                                              Around 1990 Yoccoz proved MLC at any parameter c ∈ M that is not infin-
                                           itely renormalizable (in the quadratic-like sense) thus linking the problem tightly
                                           to the Quadratic-like Renormalization Theory (see [H, M1]). First advances in this
                                           direction appeared in [L1], where MLC was established for infinitely renormaliz-
                                           able parameters of high type and the general problem was reduced, under some
                                           circumstances, to the problem of a priori bounds.
                                              Quadratic-like renormalization appears in two flavors, primitive and satellite.
                                           The above results were concerned with the primitive case. Next breakthrough in
                                           this direction appeared in the work of Jeremy Kahn [K] who established a priori
                                           bounds, and hence MLC, for all infinitely renormalizable parameters of bounded

   It took more than 10 years to prove MLC at some infinitely renormalizable

2.2. Quadratic-like maps. A quadratic-like map f : X → Y , which will also be
abbreviated as a ql map, is a holomorphic double branched covering between two
Jordan disks X ⋐ Y ⊂ C. It has a single critical point that we usually put at the
                            MLC AT FEIGENBAUM POINTS                                  9


origin 0. The annulus A = Y \ X is called the fundamental annulus of f : X → Y .
We let mod f := mod A. The filled Julia set Kf is the set of non-escaping points:
                 K ≡ Kf ≡ K(f ) := {z : f n z ∈ X, n = 0, 1, 2, . . . }.
Its boundary is called the Julia set J ≡ J(f ). The (filled) Julia set is either con-
nected or Cantor, depending on whether the critical point is non-escaping (i.e.,
0 ∈ K(f )) or otherwise. In this paper, filled Julia sets will play a bigger role than
the Julia sets, and we will often skip the adjective “filled” when it is clear (e.g.,
from the notation) what we mean.
    We say that two quadratic-like maps with connected Julia set represent the same
germ if they have the same filled Julia set and coincide in some neighborhood of
it. For a ql germ f , we define

   A simplest example of a quadratic-like map is provided by a quadratic polynomial
fc : z 7→ z 2 + c restricted to a disk Y = DR of sufficiently big radius. The Douady
and Hubbard Straightening Theorem asserts that any quadratic-like map f is hybrid
conjugate to some restricted quadratic polynomial fc . Moreover, if J is connected
then the parameter c ∈ M is unique.
   As for quadratic polynomials, two fixed points of a quadratic-like map with
connected Julia set have a different dynamical meaning. One of them, called β, is
the landing point of a proper arc γ ⊂ X \ K(f ) such that f (γ) ⊃ γ. It is either
repelling or parabolic with multiplier one. The other fixed point, called α, is either
non-repelling or a cut-point of the Julia set.

2.2.1. Combinatorial models. A quadratic polynomial f is called periodically hy-
perbolic (resp., repelling) if it does not have neutral (resp., non-repelling) cycles.
Note that in the periodically repelling case, int K = ∅, so J = K. The filled Julia set
of a periodically hyperbolic map admits a locally connected combinatorial model
Kcom = Kcom (f ) obtained by taking the quotient of the unit disk by the associ-
ated rational lamination (see [L3, §32.1.3]). The combinatorial model is endowed
with the induced dynamics fcom : Kcom → Kcom which is semi-conjugate to f by a
natural projection π : K → Kcom whose fibers are “combinatorial classes” – sets of
points inseparable by preperiodic points.

2.2.2. Hubbard continua. In the superattracting case (when the critical point is
periodic) the Hubbard tree Tf is defined as the smallest tree in K containing the
postcritical set such that Tf intersects the components of int K along internal rays.
It is invariant under f and is marked with the orbit of 0. If K is periodically
repelling and locally connected, we can define the Hubbard dendrite Tf as the
smallest connected closed forward-invariant subset of K containing the postcritical
set.

2.2.4. Little copies of M. The sets Ki in (2.4) are referred to as the little (filled)
Julia sets. Their positions in the big Julia set Kf determine the renormalization
combinatorics. By the Douady-Hubbard Straightening Theorem [DH2], the set of
parameters c for which the quadratic polynomial fc : z 7→ z 2 + c is renormalizable
with a given combinatorics forms a little Mandelbrot copy M1 ⊂ M (see [L3,
Theorem 43.1]). The renormalization combinatorics can be formally encoded by
the Hubbard tree T◦ of the superattracting center c◦ of M1 .
   Each little copy M1 can be canonically mapped onto the whole Mandelbrot set
M by the straightening homeomorphism χ1 : M1 → M.
   A little Mandelbrot copy M1 is called primitive or satellite depending on the
type of the corresponding renormalization. They can be easily distinguished as any
satellite copy is attached to some hyperbolic component of int M and does not have
the cusp at its root point.

3.2. Rectangles. A Euclidean rectangle is a rectangle Ex := [0, x] × [0, 1] ⊂ C,
where:
       • (0, 0), (x, 0), (x, 1), (0, 1) are four vertices of Ex ,
       • ∂ h Ex = [0, x] × {0, 1} is the horizontal boundary of Ex ,
       • ∂ h,0 Ex := [0, x] × {0} is the base of Ex ,
       • ∂ h,1 Ex := [0, x] × {1} is the roof of Ex ,
       • ∂ v Ex = {0, x} × [0, 1] is the vertical boundary of Ex ,
       • F(Ex ) := {{t} × [0, 1] | t ∈ [0, x]} is the vertical foliation of Ex ,
       • F full (Ex ) := {γ : [0, 1] → Ex | γ(0) ∈ ∂ h,0 Ex , γ(1) ∈ ∂ h,1 Ex } is the full
         family of curves in Ex ;
       • W(Ex ) = W(F(Ex )) = W F full (Ex ) = x is the width of Ex ,
                                                     

       • mod(Ex ) = 1/W(Ex ) = 1/x the extremal length of Ex .
    By a (topological) rectangle in a Riemann surface we mean a closed Jordan disk
R together with a conformal map h : R → Ex into the standard rectangle Ex . The
vertical foliation F(R), the full family F full (R), the base ∂ h,0 R, the roof ∂ h,1 R,
the vertices of R, and other objects are defined by pulling back the corresponding
