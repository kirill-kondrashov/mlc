# Decision on B1-R

Date: 6 November 2026.

**The formula $r_m=2^m+1$ is now proved on paper for every $m$.**
The complete proof appears below. It uses the first stabilizer's image
and a short recurrence for abelian images.

**B1-R is retired as the primary research candidate.**
It remains a complete example of problem selection and proof preparation.
An independent scientific contribution for this formula has not been established.

Two conclusions are distinguished below:

- Mathematical: the formula is proved.
- Research: the elementary consequence obtained does not provide sufficient
  grounds to choose it as the main result of a first paper.

# Complete proof of the rank formula

## Notation

Let $G=F(a,b)$, $X=\{0,1\}$, and

$$
\psi(a)=(1,b)(01),\qquad\psi(b)=(1,a).
$$

Use a right action and the rule
$(gh)|_s=(g|_s)(h|_{s^g})$.
Write $\rho_m$ for the action on $X^m$ and
$H_m=\ker\rho_m$ for the pointwise stabilizer of the whole level.
In particular, $H_0=G$.

Let $\alpha:G\to\mathbb Z^2$ record exponent sums of $a,b$,
and $\beta:G\to\mathbb Z$ the exponent sum of $b$.
For each $m$, define

$$
\Lambda_m:H_m\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{|v|\le m}\mathbb Z^2,\qquad
g\longmapsto\bigl(\alpha(g|_v)\bigr)_{|v|\le m},
$$

$$
L_m=\operatorname{im}\Lambda_m,\qquad r_m=\operatorname{rank}L_m.
$$

On $H_m$, every section of depth at most $m$ is a homomorphism:
its index is fixed in the product formula. Thus $\Lambda_m$ is a
homomorphism. Rank means the dimension of the rational linear span.
The group $L_m$ is a subgroup of a free abelian group of finite rank,
so it is free and finitely generated.

## Lemma 1. Image of the first stabilizer

The following exact equality holds:

$$
\psi(H_1)=
\{(u,v)\in G^2:\beta(u)=\beta(v)\}.
\tag{A1}
$$

**Proof.** On the first level, $a$ exchanges the two letters and $b$ fixes
them. Thus $H_1$ is defined by parity of the exponent sum of $a$,
with coset representatives $1,a$.
When reading a positive letter $a$ or $b$, the corrections
$t_\varepsilon g t_{\varepsilon'}^{-1}$ are respectively $1,b$
from parity $0$, and $a^2,aba^{-1}$ from parity $1$.
For inverse letters, use inverses of the corrections for the reversed transitions.
For a word in $H_1$, the product of corrections telescopes to the word itself.
Hence $H_1$ is generated by $b,a^2,aba^{-1}$. Their images are

$$
(1,a),\qquad(b,b),\qquad(a,1).
$$

Therefore $\psi(H_1)$ is the subgroup $S$ of $G^2$ generated by these
three pairs. Each pair has equal exponent sums of $b$, proving one inclusion.

Let $K=\ker\beta$. The elements $b^k a b^{-k}$, $k\in\mathbb Z$,
generate $K$. To verify this, read a word from left to right, move the
accumulated power of $b$ to the right, and replace each $a^{\pm1}$ by
the conjugate $b^k a^{\pm1}b^{-k}$. The remaining power of $b$ is
$\beta$ of the word. It is zero for a word in $K$.

Conjugating $(a,1)$ and $(1,a)$ by powers of $(b,b)$ shows that
$S$ contains $K\times\{1\}$ and $\{1\}\times K$, hence $K\times K$.

If $\beta(u)=\beta(v)=k$, then

$$
(u,v)=(u b^{-k},v b^{-k})(b,b)^k\in S,
$$

because $u b^{-k},v b^{-k}\in K$. This proves the reverse inclusion. $\square$

## Lemma 2. All stabilizers described by sections

For $m\ge1$,

$$
\psi(H_m)=
\{(u,v)\in H_{m-1}^2:\beta(u)=\beta(v)\}.
\tag{A2}
$$

**Proof.** An element $g$ fixes every word of length $m$ exactly when
it fixes the first level and both sections fix every word of length $m-1$.
This follows from $(sw)^g=s^g w^{g|_s}$.

Hence $\psi(H_m)=\psi(H_1)\cap H_{m-1}^2$.
For the reverse inclusion, choose a preimage of the pair in $H_1$:
both its sections belong to $H_{m-1}$, so the preimage lies in $H_m$.
Now apply (A1). $\square$

## Lemma 3. Recovering the root abelianization

For every $g\in G$,

$$
\alpha(g|_0)+\alpha(g|_1)=T\alpha(g),
\qquad T(x,y)=(y,x).
\tag{A3}
$$

**Proof.** The sum of the abelian images of sections is a homomorphism:
in a product, the permutation merely reindexes the two summands.
For $a$ the sum is $(0,1)$; for $b$ it is $(1,0)$.
These elements generate the free group, proving (A3).
Moreover, $T^{-1}=T$. $\square$

## Lemma 4. Fiber product of abelian images

Define $f_j:L_j\to\mathbb Z$ to be the $b$-coordinate of the root pair.
Then $f_j(\Lambda_j(g))=\beta(g)$, so this is well-defined.
For $m\ge1$, there is an isomorphism of abelian groups

$$
L_m\simeq
\{(p,q)\in L_{m-1}^2:f_{m-1}(p)=f_{m-1}(q)\}.
\tag{A4}
$$

**Proof.** Remove the root pair from $\Lambda_m(g)$ and separate the
remaining coordinates by their first letter. This gives a homomorphism

$$
J_m:L_m\longrightarrow L_{m-1}\oplus L_{m-1},
$$

$$
J_m(\Lambda_m(g))=
\bigl(\Lambda_{m-1}(g|_0),\Lambda_{m-1}(g|_1)\bigr).
$$

It is well-defined as a coordinate projection. By (A3), the root pair
is determined by the root pairs of the two sections. Thus $J_m$ is injective.

By (A2), its image lies in the right-hand side of (A4).
Conversely, choose $p,q$ in that side and $u,v\in H_{m-1}$ with
$\Lambda_{m-1}(u)=p$, $\Lambda_{m-1}(v)=q$.
The equality $f_{m-1}(p)=f_{m-1}(q)$ gives the **exact integer equality**
$\beta(u)=\beta(v)$. By (A2), some $g\in H_m$ has sections $u,v$.
Then $J_m(\Lambda_m(g))=(p,q)$, proving surjectivity onto the
right-hand side of (A4). $\square$

## Theorem

For every $m\ge0$,

$$
r_m=2^m+1.
\tag{A5}
$$

**Proof.** At $m=0$, $L_0=\mathbb Z^2$, so $r_0=2$.

Every $f_j$ has nonzero image. Indeed, the finite permutation $\rho_j(b)$
has a positive order $e$. Then $b^e\in H_j$ and
$f_j(\Lambda_j(b^e))=\beta(b^e)=e\ne0$.
The image of $f_j$ is a nonzero subgroup of $\mathbb Z$, hence has rank $1$.

The right-hand side of (A4) is the kernel of

$$
L_{m-1}\oplus L_{m-1}\longrightarrow
f_{m-1}(L_{m-1}),\qquad
(p,q)\longmapsto f_{m-1}(p)-f_{m-1}(q).
$$

This map is surjective: an element $f_{m-1}(p)$ is the image of $(p,0)$.
To compute the rank explicitly, write its nonzero image as $d\mathbb Z$
with $d>0$ and choose $p_0$ with $f_{m-1}(p_0)=d$.
The map $kd\mapsto(kp_0,0)$ splits the surjection.
Thus $L_{m-1}\oplus L_{m-1}$ is the direct sum of its kernel and an
infinite cyclic group. We obtain

$$
r_m=2r_{m-1}-1.
$$

Induction gives $r_m=2^m+1$. $\square$

The finite sequence $2,3,5,9$ from the initial worked example is now
explained by a general proof. The computations provide an additional
check; they are not used to infer all depths from finitely many cases.

# Why the research decision changed

The missing step was initially thought to be the general lower bound.
The full image is described by (A4), after which only the kernel rank
of one nonzero integer functional remains to be computed.

No independent difficult step has therefore been identified for B1-R
that would justify making it the primary research problem.
This assesses its role in this project; it does not imply that short
proofs cannot have scientific value.

The next layer $\gamma_3$ is not automatically new.
Theorem 4.1 of the paper already studies the corresponding images
for the faithful action group. A problem about this layer requires
an exact comparison first.

# Criterion for selecting the next research result

Before beginning the main proof cycle, prepare one completed card
for each candidate:

1. **Statement.** Objects, class, hypotheses, and conclusion.
2. **Closest results.** Number, page or section, and version of at least
   one genuinely close theorem; then check predecessors and later work.
3. **Reduction.** Try to derive the candidate from those results. If the
   reduction works, write it out completely and change the task's status.
4. **Contribution.** State the exact conclusion not covered by the checked
   theorems and explain its mathematical use.
5. **Distinguishing example.** Choose an object or regime displaying the
   proposed contribution and verify its hypotheses.
6. **One difficult lemma.** State the necessary additional step.
7. **Audit status.** List inspected sources, unchecked later work,
   and any external feedback received.

A working failure to find a predecessor is recorded as
**"novelty provisionally supported within the inspected sources."**
It is not a claim about the entire literature.

Allow two weeks to select and initially screen a candidate.
If only a broad topic or the name of the next layer remains,
the main proof cycle does not begin.
