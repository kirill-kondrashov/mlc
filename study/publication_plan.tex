\noindent\textbf{Goal.} Prove MLC for a new class of quadratic parameters
using induced-return estimates with growing losses. A possible tool is
bisets supplemented by collar geometry.

\textbf{Checked M1 reduction.} Suppose
\[
m_{j+1}\ge a_jm_j+u_j,\quad a_j\ge0,\quad
u_j\ge\beta_j/d_j,\quad K_j\ge1.
\]
If \(\beta_j\ge\eta>0\), \(d_jK_j\le C(j+1)\), and \(m_0\ge0\),
then \(\sum_jm_{j+1}/K_j\) diverges. If this budget is realized by
connected parameter pieces with the separation estimate in
\repo{mlc_bridge.pdf}, they shrink to a point and yield local connectivity.
The numerical and topological steps are proved in \repo{../Mlc/Research/}.

\section{Open statement}
Define admissible genuine quadratic returns and prove \texttt{Realization}:
construct parameter pieces with the stated separation budget.
The concrete class still needs to be selected; no extension of the published
range is established. \texttt{RootInput} separately records analytic
realization and full \texttt{Coverage}. Full coverage concerns global MLC.
The first paper should provide a new subclass and a witness outside an
exactly specified published class.

\section{First four sessions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Reproduce the harmonic loss example and check its Lean theorems.
It shows that a divergent budget is compatible with \(m_j\to0\).
The numerical example does not specify a quadratic parameter.
\item Choose genuine puzzle pieces and returns. Identify the geometric
object represented by each \(d_j,K_j,\beta_j,m_j\). This defines a
checkable admissibility predicate independent of MLC.
\item Work through the Covering Lemma on an unbounded family: disks,
collars, degrees, thresholds. Compare with existing bounded-degree blocks.
This tests the possibility of a new analytic contribution.
\item State the separation estimate and exhibit a parameter outside the
specified published class. If only a reduction to a known result emerges,
close the candidate with the reduction written out.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Workflow and reading}
After admission: analytic lemma, existence of admissible returns,
new parameter, application of the checked reduction, article draft,
and discussion. Dudko's problems provide a comparison map and are not
premises of M1. Intersections with the molecule and virtual near-Molecule
regimes require proof.

12--15 hours per week: 6 on proofs, 2 on sources, 2 on writing,
1 on checks, 1 on the log. Devote the optional three hours to the current
lemma. Read a proof together with its application. Slow reading with
selective copying of statements produces a dependency map and questions.
