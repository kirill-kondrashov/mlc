\chapter{The Connection between MLC and the Factorization Lemma}
\noindent This chapter establishes that uniform factorization of local components is equivalent to local connectivity of the Mandelbrot set. A compactness reduction turns the finite-level inclusion into the absence of “bad” points in the limit set; the converse constructs a connected neighborhood as an intersection of nested compact components. Thus uniform factorization is an exact criterion for MLC.

\section{Compactness Reduction for Factorization}
\label{sec:factorization-compactness-reduction}

For later comparison of the limit set with the finite approximations,
fix \(c\in\M\), \(0<\delta<r\), and \(N\in\N\), and set
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:factorization-local-data}
 F_N(c,r):=O_N\cap\overline B(c,r),\qquad
 \mathcal B_N(c;r,\delta):=
 \bigl(F_N(c,r)\setminus C_N(c,r)\bigr)\cap\overline B(c,\delta).
\end{equation}
The set \(F_N(c,r)\) is compact and semialgebraic. It therefore has
finitely many components, each compact, and
\(\mathcal B_N(c;r,\delta)\) is compact.

\begin{proposition}[Compactness Reduction for Factorization]
\label{prop:factorization-compactness-reduction}
For fixed \(c\in\M\), \(0<\delta<r\), and \(N\in\N\), the following
conditions are equivalent:
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:factorization-finite-inclusion}
 \exists L\ge N,\qquad
 O_L\cap\overline B(c,\delta)\subseteq C_N(c,r)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:no-bad-limit}
 \mathcal B_N(c;r,\delta)\cap\M=\varnothing.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The inclusion~\eqref{eq:factorization-finite-inclusion} and
\(\M\subseteq O_L\) imply~\eqref{eq:no-bad-limit}. Conversely, suppose
\(\mathcal B_N(c;r,\delta)\cap\M=\varnothing\). The nested compact sets
\(\mathcal B_N(c;r,\delta)\cap O_L\), \(L\ge N\), cannot all be
nonempty: their intersection is
\(\mathcal B_N(c;r,\delta)\cap\M\). Thus for some \(L\ge N\),
\(\mathcal B_N(c;r,\delta)\cap O_L=\varnothing\). Since
\(O_L\subseteq O_N\) and \(\delta<r\), this gives
\eqref{eq:factorization-finite-inclusion}.
\end{proof}

\section{Uniform Component Control and Local Connectivity}

Let \(\operatorname{Disc}(S)\) denote the discrete category with object
set \(S\). For fixed \(c\in\M\) and \(0<\delta<r\), set
\[
 \mathbf P_L(c,\delta):=
 \operatorname{Disc}\bigl(\pi_0(O_L\cap\overline B(c,\delta))\bigr),
 \qquad
 \mathbf C_N(c,r):=
 \operatorname{Disc}\bigl(\pi_0(O_N\cap\overline B(c,r))\bigr).
\]
For \(L\ge N\), the inclusion
\[
 O_L\cap\overline B(c,\delta)
 \subseteq O_N\cap\overline B(c,r)
\]
sends each connected component of the left-hand set to the unique
component of the right-hand set that contains it, and defines a functor
\[
 F_{L,N}^{c;\delta,r}:
 \mathbf P_L(c,\delta)\longrightarrow\mathbf C_N(c,r).
\]
The inclusions at successive levels also define functors
\[
 u_{L+1,L}:\mathbf P_{L+1}(c,\delta)\to\mathbf P_L(c,\delta),
\qquad
 v_{N+1,N}:\mathbf C_{N+1}(c,r)\to\mathbf C_N(c,r).
\]
For \(L\ge N\), they satisfy the identity
\[
 v_{N+1,N}\circ F_{L+1,N+1}^{c;\delta,r}
 =
 F_{L,N}^{c;\delta,r}\circ u_{L+1,L}.
\]
This identity holds because both composites send a component at the
deeper level to the component at the coarser level that contains it.

Define realization functors
\[
 \mathcal Y_L:\mathbf P_L(c,\delta)\to\mathbf{CompHaus},
 \qquad
 \mathcal X_N:\mathbf C_N(c,r)\to\mathbf{CompHaus}
\]
by sending each object to its corresponding compact component;
components of compact sets are closed and hence compact. Inclusions of
components define natural transformations
\[
 \mathcal Y_{L+1}\Rightarrow\mathcal Y_L\circ u_{L+1,L},\qquad
 \mathcal X_{N+1}\Rightarrow\mathcal X_N\circ v_{N+1,N},\qquad
 \mathcal Y_L\Rightarrow\mathcal X_N\circ F_{L,N}^{c;\delta,r}.
\]

The component \(C_N(c,r)\) containing \(c\) is a distinguished object
of \(\mathbf C_N(c,r)\). Condition \(\mathsf{UC}\) is equivalent to
requiring that, for the chosen \(r,\delta\), for every \(N\) there be
some \(L\ge N\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \forall A\in\pi_0(O_L\cap\overline B(c,\delta)),\qquad
 F_{L,N}^{c;\delta,r}(A)=C_N(c,r).
 \label{eq:component-functor-factorization}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the functor from the category of deeper local components
must factor through the full subcategory on the single distinguished
object. Commutativity of the transition functors follows from the set
inclusions; the factorization in
\eqref{eq:component-functor-factorization} is the additional geometric
assertion.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform Component Control Is Equivalent to Local Connectivity]
\label{thm:uniform-component-control}
\[
 \mathsf{UC}\leftrightarrow\LC(\M).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First suppose \(\LC(\M)\). Fix \(c\in\M\) and \(\varepsilon>0\).
Local connectivity gives a connected neighborhood \(V\) of \(c\), open
in \(\M\), such that
\[
 c\in V\subseteq \M\cap B(c,\varepsilon/2).
\]
Since \(V\) is open in \(\M\), there is \(d>0\) such that
\(\M\cap B(c,d)\subseteq V\). Set
\[
 \delta:=\min\{d/2,\varepsilon/4\},\qquad r:=3\varepsilon/4,
 \qquad W:=\overline V^{\,\M}.
\]
Then \(0<\delta<r<\varepsilon\),
\(\M\cap\overline B(c,\delta)\subseteq V\), and \(W\) is compact and
connected, contains \(c\), and lies in
\(\M\cap\overline B(c,\varepsilon/2)\subseteq\overline B(c,r)\).
Therefore \(W\subseteq C_N(c,r)\) for every \(N\), since it is a
connected subset of \(F_N(c,r)\) containing \(c\). In particular,
\[
 \mathcal B_N(c;r,\delta)\cap\M=\varnothing
 \qquad(N\in\N).
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:factorization-compactness-reduction} gives, for
each \(N\), an index \(L\ge N\) such that
\[
 O_L\cap\overline B(c,\delta)\subseteq C_N(c,r).
\]
Thus \(\mathsf{UC}\) holds.

Now suppose \(\mathsf{UC}\). Fix \(c\in\M\) and a relatively open
neighborhood \(U\) of \(c\) in \(\M\). Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) such
that \(\M\cap B(c,\varepsilon)\subseteq U\), apply
\eqref{eq:uniform-component-control}, and set
\[
 C:=\bigcap_{N\in\N}C_N(c,r).
\]
Since \(O_{N+1}\subseteq O_N\), we have
\(C_{N+1}(c,r)\subseteq C_N(c,r)\). The set
\(O_N\cap\overline B(c,r)\) is compact; its component \(C_N(c,r)\) is
closed, hence compact and connected, and contains \(c\). The theorem
on intersections of decreasing nonempty compact connected sets shows
that \(C\) is nonempty, compact, and connected. Moreover,
\(C\subseteq\bigcap_NO_N=\M\) and \(C\subseteq\overline B(c,r)\).

Let \(y\in\M\cap\overline B(c,\delta)\). For each \(N\),
\eqref{eq:uniform-component-control} gives \(L\ge N\) such that
\[
 O_L\cap\overline B(c,\delta)\subseteq C_N(c,r).
\]
Since \(y\in\M\subseteq O_L\), we have \(y\in C_N(c,r)\). This holds
for every \(N\), so
\(\M\cap\overline B(c,\delta)\subseteq C\). Hence
\[
 c\in\M\cap B(c,\delta)\subseteq C
 \subseteq\M\cap\overline B(c,r)
 \subseteq\M\cap B(c,\varepsilon)\subseteq U.
\]
Thus \(C\) is a connected neighborhood of \(c\) contained in \(U\).
Since \(c\) and \(U\) were arbitrary, \(\M\) is locally connected.
\end{proof}

Thus, realizing the distinguished objects together with the functorial
inclusions gives the connected set
\(\bigcap_N C_N(c,r)\subseteq\M\). Factorization
\eqref{eq:component-functor-factorization} ensures that this set
contains \(\M\cap\overline B(c,\delta)\), so it is a neighborhood of
\(c\) in \(\M\). Proposition~\ref{prop:equicontinuous-selection} then
gives a sequence of maps with a common modulus of continuity.

In particular, \(\mathsf{UC}\) implies \(\mathsf{EA}\) by
Proposition~\ref{prop:equicontinuous-selection}, and hence, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:diagonal-finite-scale}, gives the required finite
oscillation estimates at arbitrarily large levels. For the specific
outer sets \(O_N\), completing this route requires the geometric
factorization~\eqref{eq:component-functor-factorization}; naturality of
the functors expresses compatibility of the transitions between levels.

\section{Status of the Categorical Route in Lean}

The component categories and realization functors in this chapter are
not yet formalized in Lean. In particular, the present development does
not contain the component-set functors
\(\mathbf P_L(c,\delta)\to\mathbf C_N(c,r)\), their realizations in
\(\mathbf{CompHaus}\), or a Lean proof of the factorization
\eqref{eq:component-functor-factorization}. The AM and GC estimates and
their implication to this factorization are also not formalized.

The current Lean development formalizes related interfaces, but not
this proof route:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{MLC.Categorical.Approximation} is the over-category of
  the parameter plane, and \texttt{MLC.Categorical.ApproximationTower}
  is the type of inverse diagrams in that over-category.
  \texttt{MLC.Categorical.Mandelbrot.outerOrbitApproximationTower}
  packages the finite-orbit outer approximations as such a diagram.
  The set-level intersection theorem has not yet been identified with
  the generic categorical limit object.
  \item \texttt{MLC.Categorical.MLCConjecture} states local
  connectedness of the Mandelbrot object in \texttt{TopCat}.
  \texttt{MLC.Categorical.categorical\_mlc\_conjecture} proves this
  target from \texttt{MLC.RootInput}, whose mathematical content is
  \texttt{MLC.ParameterComponent.\allowbreak MandelbrotUniformOuterBuffer}. This
  is an explicit theorem hypothesis, not a project axiom.
  \item A separate conditional route:
  \texttt{MLC.UniformGeometry.}\\
  \texttt{mandelbrot\_locallyConnected\_of\_squareTower}
  derives local connectedness from an orbit-retraction tower. Existence
  of such a tower is not established, and this route is distinct from
  the AM/GC factorization argument.
\end{itemize}

Thus the categorical language in this document provides a precise
mathematical organization of the proposed component argument, while
Lean currently formalizes the topological target and conditional
interfaces rather than the AM/GC proof itself.
