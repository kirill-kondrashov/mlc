\section*{Meaning and position relative to the literature}

Throughout, MLC denotes the conjecture that the Mandelbrot set is
locally connected at every point.
The result below constructs continuous maps that fix successively
deeper finite-orbit outer approximations to the Mandelbrot set.  Each
map stays within an explicit \(2^{-n}\) distance of the finite-orbit
outer set that it fixes, and the images contain the Mandelbrot set
and converge to it in Hausdorff distance.  Thus finite
critical-orbit tests yield quantitative continuous approximations
of the full set.

The escape-radius description, exterior uniformization, and
Carath\'eodory criterion used below are classical
\cite{DH,Milnor}.  The full MLC conjecture remains open in the
current literature; known results establish local connectivity for
special parameter classes, including certain bounded-type satellite
parameters and Feigenbaum parameters
\cite{DudkoStatus,DudkoLyubichSat,DudkoLyubichFeigenbaum}.
The contribution claimed here is the explicit finite-depth
Green-function bound and its assembly into a map-valued proof
program.

The categorical refinement below places finite-orbit outer sets and
Green sublevels in naturally related inverse systems.  The cutoff
maps are natural with respect to a constructible sequence of split
retractions, and relative compactness of these morphisms in a mapping
space is equivalent to MLC.  Establishing that compactness would prove
the full conjecture, beyond results restricted to special parameter
classes; the present document proves the criterion, not its
compactness hypothesis.  Its priority relative to all existing
formulations remains unverified.

Step~8 characterizes summability of some cutoff sequence, while the
categorical criterion below characterizes compactness of the explicit
cutoff family.  Each criterion is equivalent to MLC.  Summability for
the selected sequence is a sufficient route and may be stronger than
MLC.
The unconditional theorem gives quantitative approximations and a
concrete proof target; these estimates provide no counterexample or
obstruction to MLC.

\section*{Strongest unconditional theorem proved below}

Set
\[
 \N:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\},\qquad f_c(z):=z^2+c,
 \qquad p_0(c):=0,\qquad p_{n+1}(c):=p_n(c)^2+c,
\]
\[
 \M:=\{c\in\C:\sup_{n\in\N}|p_n(c)|<\infty\},\qquad
 K:=\overline B(0,2),\qquad
 \D:=\{x+iy:x,y\in[-2,2]\},
\]
\[
 O_N:=K\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{N}p_j^{-1}(K),\qquad
 \Pi(z):=\frac{2z}{\max\{2,|z|\}}.
\]
For a nonempty set \(A\subseteq\C\), let
\[
 \dist(z,A):=\inf_{a\in A}|z-a|.
\]
For nonempty compact \(A,B\subseteq\C\), let
\[
 \dH(A,B):=
 \max\left\{\sup_{a\in A}\dist(a,B),\
             \sup_{b\in B}\dist(b,A)\right\}.
\]
Write \(C(X,Y)\) for the set of continuous maps from \(X\) to \(Y\).

\begin{summarytheorem}[Constructed outer-map approximation]
There exist \(\lambda\in\N^\N\) and
\(T_\bullet\in C(\D,\D)^\N\) with
\begin{gather}
 \lambda_0=0,\qquad\lambda_1=1,\qquad
 \forall n\in\N,\quad \lambda_n\ge n,\quad
 \lambda_{n+1}>\lambda_n,\label{eq:opening-depths}\\
 T_0=\id_{\D},\qquad T_1=\Pi|_{\D},\qquad
 \forall n\ge1,\quad T_n(\D)\subseteq K,\label{eq:opening-maps}\\
 \forall n\in\N\ \forall c\in O_{\lambda_n},\quad
                  T_n(c)=c,\label{eq:opening-fixing}\\
 \forall n\in\N,\quad
 \sup_{z\in\D}\dist(T_n(z),O_{\lambda_n})\le2^{-n},
 \label{eq:opening-proximity}\\
 \forall n\in\N,\quad \M\subseteq T_n(\D),\qquad
 \forall n\ge2,\quad \dH(T_n(\D),\M)\le2^{-n}.
 \label{eq:opening-image-gap}
\end{gather}
\end{summarytheorem}
\noindent
The maps fix their finite-orbit outer sets, satisfy uniform
proximity bounds, and have images converging to \(\M\) in Hausdorff
distance.  The first proof route additionally requires summability
of successive map movements, studied in Steps~7--8 and isolated in
Step~13.

\section{Stepwise construction}

\subsection*{Step 1. Identify the target and its finite-orbit outer sets.}

\begin{lemma}[Escape radius and outer-set properties]
\label{lem:outer-sets}
\begin{gather}
 \M=\{c\in\C:\forall n\in\N,\ |p_n(c)|\le2\},\label{eq:escape}\\
 \M\subseteq O_{N+1}\subseteq O_N\subseteq K\subseteq\D,\qquad
 \bigcap_{N\in\N}O_N=\M.\label{eq:outer-nesting}
\end{gather}
The sets \(\M,O_N,K,\D\) are compact and closed, and \(\D\) is convex.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(q:=|p_n(c)|>2\) and \(|c|\le q\).  Induction gives
\[
 |p_{n+j}(c)|\ge q(q-1)^j\qquad(j\in\N).
\]
For the induction step, if \(r:=|p_{n+j}(c)|\ge q\), then
\[
 |p_{n+j+1}(c)|
 \ge r^2-|c|
 \ge r^2-r
 \ge(q-1)r.
\]
Thus the orbit is unbounded.  If \(|c|>2\), use \(n=1\) and
\(q=|p_1(c)|=|c|\).  If \(|c|\le2\) and an iterate has modulus
greater than two, use that iterate as \(q\).  This proves one
direction of \eqref{eq:escape}; the other follows by taking the
bound \(B=2\).

Each \(p_j\) is continuous.  Hence each \(O_N\) is a finite
intersection of closed sets and is a closed subset of the compact
set \(K\).  The identities \(p_0(c)=0\) and \(p_1(c)=c\) give
\(O_{N+1}\subseteq O_N\subseteq K\).  Equation~\eqref{eq:escape}
gives \(\M\subseteq O_N\) and
\[
 \bigcap_{N\in\N}O_N
 =K\cap\bigcap_{j\in\N}p_j^{-1}(K)=\M.
\]
The last equality also makes \(\M\) closed in \(K\), hence compact.
Finally, \(K\subseteq\D\), and \(\D\) is a closed, bounded, convex
square.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Step 2. Prove Hausdorff convergence of the outer sets.}

\begin{definition}[Outer-set gap]\label{def:outer-gap}
For \(N\in\N\), define
\[
 h_N:=\dH(O_N,\M)
     =\sup_{z\in O_N}\dist(z,\M).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Outer-set gap tends to zero]\label{thm:outer-gap}
\[
 0\le h_{N+1}\le h_N,\qquad \lim_{N\to\infty}h_N=0.
\]
Define
\begin{gather}
 \ell_0:=0,\qquad\ell_1:=1,\qquad
 \ell_n:=\min\{N\in\N:N>\ell_{n-1},\ N\ge n,\ h_N\le2^{-n}\}
 \quad(n\ge2),\label{eq:lower-depths}\\
 \Lambda:=
 \{\lambda\in\N^\N:
   \lambda_0=0,\ \lambda_1=1,\
   \forall n,\ \lambda_n\ge\ell_n,\
   \lambda_{n+1}>\lambda_n\}.
 \label{eq:depth-class}
\end{gather}
Then each minimum in \eqref{eq:lower-depths} exists,
\(\ell\in\Lambda\), and
\[
 \forall\lambda\in\Lambda\ \forall n\ge2,\qquad
 \lambda_n\ge n,\quad h_{\lambda_n}\le2^{-n}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Nesting gives \(h_{N+1}\le h_N\).  Let \(h_\infty\) be the limit.
For each \(N\), choose \(z_N\in O_N\) with
\(\dist(z_N,\M)=h_N\).  If \(h_\infty>0\), compactness of \(O_0\)
gives a subsequence \(z_{N_k}\to z_\infty\in O_0\).
For every fixed \(m\), eventually \(N_k\ge m\), so
\(z_{N_k}\in O_m\).  Closedness gives
\(z_\infty\in\bigcap_mO_m=\M\).  Continuity of distance to a
nonempty set then gives
\[
 h_{N_k}=\dist(z_{N_k},\M)\longrightarrow0,
\]
contrary to \(h_{N_k}\to h_\infty>0\).  Thus \(h_N\to0\).
The required integers \(\ell_n\) now exist recursively.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Step 3. Introduce the exterior conformal coordinate.}

\begin{theorem}[Classical exterior uniformization]\label{thm:uniformization}
\(\M\) is nondegenerate, connected, and has connected complement.
There are inverse conformal maps
\[
 \Phi:\C\setminus\M\longrightarrow\{w\in\C:|w|>1\},
 \qquad \Psi:=\Phi^{-1},\qquad
 \frac{\Phi(c)}{c}\longrightarrow1\quad(|c|\to\infty).
\]
For \(k\in\N\), define
\[
 f_c^{\circ0}:=\id_{\C},\qquad
 f_c^{\circ(k+1)}:=f_c\circ f_c^{\circ k},\qquad
 g_c(z):=\lim_{k\to\infty}2^{-k}
             \log\max\{1,|f_c^{\circ k}(z)|\}.
\]
Define
\[
 G(c):=
 \begin{cases}
  \log|\Phi(c)|,&c\notin\M,\\
  0,&c\in\M.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then
\begin{gather}
 G\in C(\C,[0,\infty)),\qquad G^{-1}(0)=\M,\qquad
 G(c)-\log|c|\longrightarrow0\quad(|c|\to\infty),\label{eq:green}\\
 g_c(f_c(z))=2g_c(z),\qquad G(c)=g_c(c)=2g_c(0).
 \label{eq:green-normalization}
\end{gather}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The connectedness and exterior uniformization are
\href{https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hubbard/OrsayEnglish.pdf#page=64}
{Douady--Hubbard, Chapter 8, Theorem 8.1, pp.~64--65},
and
\href{https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hubbard/OrsayEnglish.pdf#page=65}
{Corollary 8.3, p.~65}.
Continuity of the extended dynamical Green function is
\href{https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hubbard/OrsayEnglish.pdf#page=62}
{Proposition 8.1(b), p.~62}.
The parameter normalization is
\href{https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hubbard/OrsayEnglish.pdf#page=65}
{Corollary 8.4(a), p.~65}.
The functional equation follows by shifting the index in the
defining limit.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Step 4. Define positive-potential cutoffs.}

\begin{definition}[Equipotentials and cutoff maps]\label{def:cutoffs}
For \(s>0\), define
\begin{gather}
 E_s:=\{c\in\C:G(c)\le s\},\qquad
 \kappa(s):=\dH(E_s,\M),\qquad
 \Gamma_s(\theta):=\Psi(e^{s+i\theta}),\label{eq:equipotentials}\\
 P_s(c):=
 \begin{cases}
  c,&G(c)\le s,\\
  \displaystyle\Psi\left(e^s\frac{\Phi(c)}{|\Phi(c)|}\right),
       &G(c)>s,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad Q_s:=\Pi\circ P_s|_{\D}.
 \label{eq:cutoffs}
\end{gather}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Positive-level cutoff properties]\label{thm:cutoff-properties}
For \(0<s<t\),
\begin{gather}
 E_s\subseteq\operatorname{int}E_t,\qquad
 \bigcap_{s>0}E_s=\M,\qquad
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}\kappa(s)=0,\label{eq:equipotential-gap}\\
 P_s(\C)=E_s,\qquad P_s|_{E_s}=\id_{E_s},\qquad
 P_s\circ P_t=P_{\min\{s,t\}},\label{eq:cutoff-semigroup}\\
 Q_s\in C(\D,\D),\qquad Q_s|_\M=\id_\M,\qquad
 \dH(Q_s(\D),\M)\le\kappa(s).\label{eq:Q-gap}
\end{gather}
The boundary of \(E_s\) is the regular real-analytic Jordan curve
\(\Gamma_s\).  The map \((s,c)\mapsto P_s(c)\) is continuous on
\((0,\infty)\times\C\).  For every \(s>0\),
\[
 Q_s(\D)\setminus\M\ne\varnothing.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Continuity and properness of \(G\), together with \(G^{-1}(0)=\M\),
give compactness, nesting, and the intersection identity.
If \(\kappa(s)\not\to0\), choose \(s_j\downarrow0\) and
\(c_j\in E_{s_j}\) at a fixed positive distance from \(\M\).
Compactness of \(E_{s_1}\) gives a convergent subsequence with
limit in \(G^{-1}(0)=\M\), a contradiction.

The map \(\Gamma_s\) is injective on the circle and
\[
 \Gamma_s'(\theta)=ie^{s+i\theta}\Psi'(e^{s+i\theta})\ne0.
\]
The unbounded complementary component of this Jordan curve is
\(\Psi(\{|w|>e^s\})=\{G>s\}\).  The Jordan curve theorem identifies
its filled interior with \(E_s\).

For \(c\notin\M\), the cutoff has the equivalent formula
\[
 P_s(c)=
 \Psi\left(e^{\min\{s,G(c)\}}\frac{\Phi(c)}{|\Phi(c)|}\right).
\]
This proves continuity away from \(\M\), including across \(G=s\).
At \((s_0,c_0)\) with \(c_0\in\M\), continuity of \(G\) gives a
neighborhood with \(G(c)<s_0/2\).  For \(s\) near \(s_0\), \(P_s\)
is the identity there.  The semigroup identity follows by replacing
the potential \(G(c)\) by \(\min\{s,G(c)\}\), without changing
the external angle.

The map \(\Pi\) is the nonexpansive metric projection onto \(K\)
and fixes \(\M\subseteq K\).  Hence
\[
 \dist(\Pi(z),\M)\le\dist(z,\M).
\]
Since \(P_s(\D)\subseteq E_s\) and \(Q_s\) fixes \(\M\),
\eqref{eq:Q-gap} follows.

Finally, \(\M\) is a nonempty proper closed subset of the connected
set \(K\), since \(2\in K\setminus\M\).  The relatively open set
\(K\cap\{G<s\}\) contains \(\M\) and cannot equal \(\M\).
Choose \(c\in K\cap\{G<s\}\setminus\M\).  Then \(Q_s(c)=c\),
so \(c\in Q_s(\D)\setminus\M\).
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Step 5. Bound the potential of every finite-orbit outer set.}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-depth Green bound]\label{lem:green-depth}
For every \(N\in\N\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:green-depth-bound}
 \sup_{c\in O_N}G(c)\le2^{1-N}\log3.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depth-cutoff}
 2^{1-N}\log3\le s
 \Longrightarrow O_N\subseteq E_s,\qquad
 Q_s|_{O_N}=\id_{O_N},\qquad h_N\le\kappa(s).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(|c|\le2\) and \(|z|\le2\), induction gives
\[
 |f_c^{\circ k}(z)|\le3^{2^k}-1.
\]
The base case is \(k=0\).  If \(A=3^{2^k}\), the next case follows
from \((A-1)^2+2\le A^2-1\).
Consequently \(g_c(z)\le\log3\) for such \(c,z\).
For \(c\in O_N\), both \(c\) and \(p_N(c)\) belong to \(K\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:green-normalization} gives
\[
 G(c)=2g_c(0)=2^{1-N}g_c(p_N(c))
                         \le2^{1-N}\log3.
\]
This proves \(O_N\subseteq E_s\) under the stated inequality.
Since \(O_N\subseteq K\), both \(P_s\) and \(\Pi\) fix \(O_N\).
The inclusion \(O_N\subseteq E_s\) gives \(h_N\le\kappa(s)\).
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Step 6. Construct maps with shrinking image gaps.}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit exterior candidates]\label{thm:exterior-candidates}
Define
\begin{gather}
 a:=1+\max_{z\in\D}G(z),\qquad j_1:=0,\qquad s_1:=a,\notag\\
 j_n:=\min\{j\in\N:j>j_{n-1},\
                       \kappa(a2^{-j})\le2^{-n}\},\qquad
 s_n:=a2^{-j_n}\quad(n\ge2),\label{eq:height-choice}\\
 \lambda_0:=0,\qquad\lambda_1:=1,\notag\\
 \lambda_n:=\min\{N\in\N:N>\lambda_{n-1},\
                N\ge n,\ 2^{1-N}\log3\le s_n\}\quad(n\ge2),
 \label{eq:candidate-depth-choice}\\
 T_0:=\id_{\D},\qquad T_1:=\Pi|_{\D}=Q_a,\qquad
 T_n:=Q_{s_n}\quad(n\ge2).\label{eq:candidate-maps}
\end{gather}
Then \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), \(T_n\in C(\D,\D)\), and all assertions
of the opening theorem hold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The minima defining \(j_n\) exist by
\(\kappa(s)\to0\); hence \(s_n\downarrow0\).
The minima defining \(\lambda_n\) exist because
\(2^{1-N}\log3\to0\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:green-depth} gives
\[
 h_{\lambda_n}\le\kappa(s_n)\le2^{-n}\qquad(n\ge2).
\]
The recursive definition and \eqref{eq:lower-depths} give
\(\lambda_n\ge\ell_n\), so \(\lambda\in\Lambda\).

Since \(G<a\) on \(\D\), \(P_a|_{\D}=\id_{\D}\) and
\(Q_a=\Pi|_{\D}\).  At indices zero and one,
\(O_0=O_1=K\); the identity and \(\Pi\) fix \(K\).
For \(n\ge2\), \eqref{eq:depth-cutoff} gives
\(T_n|_{O_{\lambda_n}}=\id\).
For \(z\in\D\),
\[
 \dist(T_n(z),O_{\lambda_n})
 \le\dist(T_n(z),\M)
 \le\kappa(s_n)\le2^{-n}.
\]
Distance to the compact set \(O_{\lambda_n}\) is attained.
At \(n=0\), \(\dist(z,O_0)\le2\sqrt2-2<1\); at \(n=1\),
\(T_1(\D)=O_1=K\).
Every \(T_n\) fixes \(\M\), so \(\M\subseteq T_n(\D)\).
Finally, for \(n\ge2\),
\[
 \dH(T_n(\D),\M)\le\kappa(s_n)\le2^{-n}.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Step 7. Estimate motion on positive-potential bands.}

\begin{lemma}[Exterior differential equation and band estimate]
\label{lem:band-motion}
For \(s>0\), define
\[
 V(s):=\max_{\theta\in\R/2\pi\mathbb Z}
                     e^s|\Psi'(e^{s+i\theta})|.
\]
Then \(V\) is finite and continuous on \((0,\infty)\).  The curve
\(c_\theta(s):=\Psi(e^{s+i\theta})\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:external-ray-ode}
 \frac{dc_\theta}{ds}
 =\frac{\Phi(c_\theta)}{\Phi'(c_\theta)}
 =\frac{\nabla G(c_\theta)}{|\nabla G(c_\theta)|^2},
 \qquad |\nabla G(c_\theta)|
       =\frac{|\Phi'(c_\theta)|}{|\Phi(c_\theta)|}>0.
\end{equation}
For \(0<s<t\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-band-motion}
 \|Q_t-Q_s\|_\infty
 \le\sup_{c\in\C}|P_t(c)-P_s(c)|
 \le\int_s^t V(u)\,du.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The maximum defining \(V(s)\) is over a compact circle on which
\(\Psi'\) is continuous and nonzero.  Differentiating
\(\Phi(c_\theta(s))=e^{s+i\theta}\) proves the first equality
in \eqref{eq:external-ray-ode}.  The gradient identity follows
from \(G=\log|\Phi|\) and the Cauchy--Riemann equations.
If \(G(c)\le s\), both cutoffs fix \(c\).  Otherwise their values
lie on the same external ray between heights \(s\) and
\(\min\{t,G(c)\}\).  Integration of the ray speed proves the second
inequality in \eqref{eq:finite-band-motion}; nonexpansiveness of
\(\Pi\) proves the first.
\end{proof}

For the heights of Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-candidates},
\(s_1=a\), \(s_n\downarrow0\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:candidate-partial-motion}
 \sum_{n=1}^{N}\|Q_{s_{n+1}}-Q_{s_n}\|_\infty
 \le\int_{s_{N+1}}^aV(u)\,du
 \qquad(N\ge1).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
 \int_0^aV(u)\,du<\infty
 \Longrightarrow
 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|Q_{s_{n+1}}-Q_{s_n}\|_\infty<\infty.
\]

\subsection*{Step 8. Characterize the terminal summability condition.}

\begin{definition}[Local connectedness]\label{def:local-connectedness}
For \(X\subseteq\C\), define
\[
 \LC(X):=
 \left[
 \begin{gathered}
  \forall c\in X\ \forall\eta>0\ \exists\delta>0\
  \exists V\subseteq X:\\
  V\text{ is connected},\qquad
  X\cap B(c,\delta)\subseteq V\subseteq X\cap B(c,\eta)
 \end{gathered}\right].
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Summable cutoff motion is equivalent to MLC]
\label{thm:summable-cutoff}
With \(a\) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-candidates},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:summable-cutoff-equivalence}
 \LC(\M)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \left[
 \begin{gathered}
  \exists(s_n)_{n\ge1}\subset(0,\infty):\
  s_1=a,\quad s_{n+1}<s_n,\quad s_n\to0,\\
  \forall n\ge2,\quad\kappa(s_n)\le2^{-n},\\
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
       \|Q_{s_{n+1}}-Q_{s_n}\|_\infty<\infty
 \end{gathered}\right].
\end{equation}
Every sequence on the right converges uniformly to a continuous
retraction \(R:\D\to\M\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose the sequence on the right exists and set
\(b_n:=\|Q_{s_{n+1}}-Q_{s_n}\|_\infty\).
For \(m>n\),
\[
 \|Q_{s_m}-Q_{s_n}\|_\infty\le\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}b_j.
\]
Thus \(Q_{s_n}\) converges uniformly to \(R\in C(\D,\D)\).
Each \(Q_{s_n}\) fixes \(\M\), and
\[
 \dist(Q_{s_n}(z),\M)\le\kappa(s_n)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(\M\) is closed, \(R(\D)\subseteq\M\); since \(R|_\M=\id\),
\(R(\D)=\M\).

Fix \(c\in\M\) and \(\eta>0\).  Choose \(\delta>0\) so that
\[
 z\in\D,\quad |z-c|<\delta
 \Longrightarrow |R(z)-c|<\eta.
\]
The set \(W:=\D\cap B(c,\delta)\) is convex and contains \(c\).
Hence \(V:=R(W)\) is connected, and
\[
 \M\cap B(c,\delta)\subseteq V\subseteq\M\cap B(c,\eta).
\]
Thus \(\LC(\M)\).

Conversely, suppose \(\LC(\M)\).  The Carath\'eodory boundary
criterion in
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9201272#page=84}
{Milnor, Theorem 16.6, PDF p.~84}
gives a continuous extension \(\widehat\Psi\) of \(\Psi\) to
\(\{|w|\ge1\}\).  On the compact annulus
\(A_a:=\{w:1\le|w|\le e^a\}\), let
\[
 \omega_a(r):=
 \sup_{\substack{w,v\in A_a\\|w-v|\le r}}
                   |\widehat\Psi(w)-\widehat\Psi(v)|.
\]
Uniform continuity gives \(\omega_a(r)\to0\) as \(r\downarrow0\).
Define
\[
 R(c):=
 \begin{cases}
  c,&c\in\M,\\
  \displaystyle\widehat\Psi\left(
             \frac{\Phi(c)}{|\Phi(c)|}\right),&c\notin\M.
 \end{cases}
\]
For \(0<s\le a\) and \(c\notin\M\), put
\(\xi=\Phi(c)/|\Phi(c)|\) and \(r=\exp(\min\{s,G(c)\})\).
Then
\[
 |P_s(c)-R(c)|
 =|\widehat\Psi(r\xi)-\widehat\Psi(\xi)|
 \le\omega_a(e^s-1).
\]
The difference vanishes on \(\M\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-cutoff-limit}
 \sup_{c\in\C}|P_s(c)-R(c)|\le\omega_a(e^s-1).
\end{equation}
Therefore \(P_s\to R\) uniformly on \(\C\), and
\[
 \|Q_s-R|_{\D}\|_\infty\le\omega_a(e^s-1)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Choose recursively \(s_1=a\) and
\[
 0<s_n<\min\{s_{n-1}/2,2^{-n}\},\qquad
 \kappa(s_n)\le2^{-n},\qquad
 \|Q_{s_n}-R\|_\infty\le2^{-n}\quad(n\ge2).
\]
Then
\[
 \|Q_{s_{n+1}}-Q_{s_n}\|_\infty
 \le2^{-(n+1)}+2^{-n}\qquad(n\ge2),
\]
and the single term at \(n=1\) is finite.  This proves the reverse
implication.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Constructible inverse systems and a mapping-space criterion}

Write \(\mathbf{CompHaus}\) for the category of compact Hausdorff
spaces and continuous maps.  Let \(I\) be the ordered category with
objects \(n\ge2\) and a unique morphism \(n\to m\) when \(n\le m\).
For the depths and heights in Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-candidates},
put
\[
 O_n^\lambda:=O_{\lambda_n},\qquad E_n:=E_{s_n}\qquad(n\ge2).
\]
The inclusions define inverse systems
\[
 \mathcal O,\mathcal E:I^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{CompHaus},
 \qquad \mathcal O(n)=O_n^\lambda,\quad\mathcal E(n)=E_n.
\]
The finite-depth Green bound gives \(O_n^\lambda\subseteq E_n\);
these inclusions form a natural transformation \(j:\mathcal O\to
\mathcal E\).  Since \(\lambda_n\to\infty\) and \(s_n\downarrow0\),
\[
 \varprojlim_{I^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathcal O
      \cong\M\cong\varprojlim_{I^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathcal E,
 \qquad
 \iota_n^{\mathcal E}\circ j_{n+1}
       =j_n\circ\iota_n^{\mathcal O}.
\]
Here \(\iota_n^{\mathcal O}\) and \(\iota_n^{\mathcal E}\) are the
bonding inclusions from level \(n+1\) to level \(n\).
Under these identifications, \(\varprojlim j=\id_\M\).

Define the cutoff morphisms
\[
 \mathsf P_n:=P_{s_n}|_{\D}:\D\longrightarrow E_n.
\]
Regard \(\mathsf P_n\) as a map to \(E_a\) by inclusion.
For \(n\le m\), define
\[
 r_{n,m}:=P_{s_m}|_{E_n}:E_n\longrightarrow E_m.
\]
The semigroup law \(P_s\circ P_t=P_{\min\{s,t\}}\) gives
\[
 r_{n,n}=\id_{E_n},\qquad
 r_{m,k}\circ r_{n,m}=r_{n,k},\qquad
 r_{n,m}\circ\mathsf P_n=\mathsf P_m
 \quad(n\le m\le k).
\]
Thus the \(r_{n,m}\) define a covariant functor
\(\mathcal R:I\to\mathbf{CompHaus}\), and
\(\mathsf P_\bullet\) is a natural transformation from the constant
\(\D\)-diagram to \(\mathcal R\).  Each adjacent \(r_{n,n+1}\) is a
retraction of the inclusion \(E_{n+1}\hookrightarrow E_n\).
\[
 r_{n,n+1}\circ\iota_n^{\mathcal E}=\id_{E_{n+1}}.
\]
We call these functorially generated finite-stage data a
\emph{constructible cutoff sequence}.

The same maps are an approximate cone over the inverse system
\(\mathcal E\).  Define its naturality defect by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff-cone-defect}
 d_n:=
 \sup_{z\in\D}
 \left|\mathsf P_n(z)-\iota_n^{\mathcal E}
                       \bigl(\mathsf P_{n+1}(z)\bigr)\right|
 =\sup_{z\in\D}|\mathsf P_n(z)-\mathsf P_{n+1}(z)|.
\end{equation}
The equalities \(r_{n,n+1}\mathsf P_n=\mathsf P_{n+1}\) hold
exactly.  The quantities \(d_n\) measure the failure of the same
sequence to be a cone for the inverse-limit bonding inclusions.

\begin{theorem}[Categorical compactness criterion for the cutoffs]
\label{thm:categorical-cutoff-compactness}
For the heights in Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-candidates},
\[
 \LC(\M)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \overline{\{\mathsf P_n:n\ge2\}}^{\,C(\D,E_a)}
 \text{ is compact},
\]
where \(C(\D,E_a)\) has the uniform (equivalently, compact-open)
topology.  Equivalently, the constructible cutoff sequence is
equicontinuous.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The range of every \(\mathsf P_n\) lies in the compact set \(E_a\).
If \(\LC(\M)\), the boundary extension in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:summable-cutoff} gives
\[
 \|\mathsf P_n-R|_{\D}\|_\infty
 \le\omega_a(e^{s_n}-1)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the family has compact closure.

Conversely, compactness of the closure gives a uniformly convergent
subsequence \(\mathsf P_{n_j}\to R\), with \(n_j\to\infty\).
For each fixed \(k\ge2\), eventually \(n_j\ge k\), and hence
\(\mathsf P_{n_j}(\D)\subseteq E_{n_j}\subseteq E_k\).
Closedness of \(E_k\) gives \(R(\D)\subseteq E_k\), so
\[
 R(\D)\subseteq\bigcap_{k\ge2}E_k=\M.
\]
Every \(\mathsf P_n\) fixes \(\M\), so \(R|_{\M}=\id_\M\) and
\(R(\D)=\M\).  The limit coordinates form a cone over
\(\mathcal E\), hence a morphism
\(\D\to\varprojlim_{I^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathcal E\cong\M\).
Since \(\D\) is convex,
the resulting retraction implies \(\LC(\M)\), by the retraction
argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:summable-cutoff}.  The limit diagram
identifies the target; local connectedness follows from the retraction
of the locally connected square.
\end{proof}

\noindent
This mapping-space formulation is functorial: for compact Hausdorff
objects \(X,Y\) and continuous maps \(u:X\to\D\), \(v:E_a\to Y\),
the induced map \(F\mapsto v\circ F\circ u\) on morphism spaces is
continuous and preserves relative compactness.  Cofinal
reindexings preserve the inverse-system diagrams, and under MLC all
such cutoff subsequences converge to the same retraction.

\noindent
The constructible sequence also satisfies
\[
 \mathsf P_n(\D)\subseteq E_n,\qquad
 \mathsf P_n|_{O_n^\lambda}=\id,\qquad
 \M\subseteq\mathsf P_n(\D),\qquad
 \dH(\mathsf P_n(\D),\M)\le\kappa(s_n)\le2^{-n}.
\]
Summable cone defect \(\sum_{n\ge2}d_n<\infty\) is a sufficient
certificate for the compactness criterion: it makes
\(\mathsf P_n\) uniformly Cauchy.  The finite-band estimate gives
\[
 d_n\le\int_{s_{n+1}}^{s_n}V(u)\,du,
\]
so \(\int_0^aV(u)\,du<\infty\) is a stronger sufficient analytic
target.  The categorical criterion itself is equivalent to MLC;
its validity for the explicit cutoff family remains the unresolved
problem.

\noindent
The summability assertion for the particular heights selected in
Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-candidates} implies MLC.
Under MLC, the equivalence supplies a summable height selection;
that selection may differ from the explicit candidate heights.
The sufficient integral condition in
\eqref{eq:candidate-partial-motion} is a concrete analytic target
for proving summability of those candidates.

\subsection*{Step 9. Convert summable candidates into the uniform tower.}

\begin{definition}[Uniform geometric existence]\label{def:ug}
For \(\nu\in\N^\N\) and \(R_\bullet\in C(\D,\D)^\N\), define
\begin{align}
 \mathsf A(\nu,R_\bullet):={}&(R_0=\id_{\D})\nonumber\\
 &{}\land(\forall n,\ \nu_n\ge n)\nonumber\\
 &{}\land(\forall n\ \forall c\in O_{\nu_n},\ R_n(c)=c)\nonumber\\
 &{}\land\left(\forall n\ \forall z\in\D\
       \exists w\in O_{\nu_n},\ |R_n(z)-w|\le2^{-n}\right)\nonumber\\
 &{}\land\left(\forall n,\
       \|R_{n+1}-R_n\|_\infty\le8\,2^{-n}\right),
 \label{eq:UG-clauses}\\
 \mathsf{UG}:={}&
 \exists\nu\in\N^\N\ \exists R_\bullet\in C(\D,\D)^\N,\
                       \mathsf A(\nu,R_\bullet).\label{eq:UG}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Summable candidate maps imply \(\mathsf{UG}\)]
\label{thm:candidate-to-ug}
Suppose \(\nu\in\N^\N\), \(T_\bullet\in C(\D,\D)^\N\), and
\begin{gather}
 \forall n,\quad\nu_n\ge n,\label{eq:candidate-depths}\\
 \forall n\ \forall c\in O_{\nu_n},\quad T_n(c)=c,\label{eq:candidate-fixes}\\
 a_n:=\sup_{z\in\D}\dist(T_n(z),O_{\nu_n})\longrightarrow0,\label{eq:candidate-error}\\
 \sum_{n=0}^\infty b_n<\infty,\qquad
 b_n:=\|T_{n+1}-T_n\|_\infty.\label{eq:candidate-sum}
\end{gather}
Then \(\mathsf{UG}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set \(s_j:=\sum_{k=j}^\infty b_k\).  Since \(s_j\to0\) and
\(a_j\to0\), choose strictly increasing \(j_n\ge n\) so that
\[
 a_{j_n}\le2^{-n},\qquad s_{j_n}\le8\,2^{-n}\quad(n\ge1).
\]
Set \(\nu'_0=0\), \(R_0=\id_{\D}\), and
\(\nu'_n=\nu_{j_n}\), \(R_n=T_{j_n}\) for \(n\ge1\).
The fixing and depth clauses follow from
\eqref{eq:candidate-depths}--\eqref{eq:candidate-fixes}.
Compactness of \(O_{\nu'_n}\) turns the distance bound into the
point \(w\) in \eqref{eq:UG-clauses}.
For \(n\ge1\),
\[
 \|R_{n+1}-R_n\|_\infty\le s_{j_n}\le8\,2^{-n}.
\]
At \(n=0\), \(O_0=K\) and
\[
 \sup_{z\in\D}\dist(z,K)\le2\sqrt2-2<1,\qquad
 \|T_{j_1}-\id_{\D}\|_\infty\le\operatorname{diam}(\D)=4\sqrt2<8.
\]
Thus \(\mathsf A(\nu',R_\bullet)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The tower limit is a retraction and implies MLC]
\label{thm:ug-to-mlc}
\[
 \forall\nu\in\N^\N\ \forall R_\bullet\in C(\D,\D)^\N,\quad
 \mathsf A(\nu,R_\bullet)\Longrightarrow
 \exists R\in C(\D,\D):
 \begin{cases}
  \forall n,\quad\|R_n-R\|_\infty\le16\,2^{-n},\\
  R(\D)=\M,\qquad R|_\M=\id_\M.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently, \(\mathsf{UG}\Longrightarrow\LC(\M)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(m>n\), \eqref{eq:UG-clauses} gives
\[
 \|R_m-R_n\|_\infty
 \le8\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}2^{-j}\le16\,2^{-n}.
\]
Hence \(R_n\) converges uniformly to a continuous \(R:\D\to\D\)
and the displayed tail estimate holds.
Fix \(z\in\D\) and \(k\in\N\).  For \(n\ge k\), choose
\(w_n\in O_{\nu_n}\) with \(|R_n(z)-w_n|\le2^{-n}\).
Since \(\nu_n\ge n\ge k\), \(O_{\nu_n}\subseteq O_k\), and
\[
 |R(z)-w_n|\le17\,2^{-n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Closedness of \(O_k\) gives \(R(z)\in O_k\).  Thus
\(R(\D)\subseteq\bigcap_kO_k=\M\).
For \(c\in\M\), every \(R_n\) fixes \(c\); hence \(R(c)=c\).
Therefore \(R(\D)=\M\).

For \(c\in\M\) and \(\eta>0\), choose \(\delta>0\) such that
\[
 z\in\D,\quad|z-c|<\delta
 \Longrightarrow |R(z)-c|<\eta.
\]
The set \(W=\D\cap B(c,\delta)\) is convex and contains \(c\).
Thus \(V=R(W)\) is connected, and
\[
 \M\cap B(c,\delta)\subseteq V\subseteq\M\cap B(c,\eta).
\]
This is \(\LC(\M)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The uniform geometric hypothesis is equivalent to MLC]
\label{cor:ug-equivalent-mlc}
\[
 \mathsf{UG}\Longleftrightarrow\LC(\M).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\(\mathsf{UG}\Rightarrow\LC(\M)\) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:ug-to-mlc}.
For the converse, take \((s_n)\) from
Theorem~\ref{thm:summable-cutoff} and set
\[
 T_0=\id_{\D},\qquad T_1=\Pi|_{\D},\qquad
 T_n=Q_{s_n}\quad(n\ge2).
\]
For \(n\ge2\), choose recursively
\[
 \nu_0=0,\quad\nu_1=1,\quad
 \nu_n:=\min\{N\in\N:N>\nu_{n-1},\ N\ge n,\
                         2^{1-N}\log3\le s_n\}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:green-depth} gives the fixing and proximity clauses.
The sum in \eqref{eq:summable-cutoff-equivalence}, together with
\(\|\Pi-\id_{\D}\|_\infty=2\sqrt2-2<1\) gives
\eqref{eq:candidate-sum}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:candidate-to-ug} gives \(\mathsf{UG}\).
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Step 10. Organize the tower as finite compatible certificates.}

\begin{definition}[Shared continuity scales and finite towers]
\label{def:finite-towers}
Define
\[
 \Delta:=
 \{\delta\in(0,\infty)^\N:
   \forall k,\ \delta_k\le2^{-k},\
   \delta_{k+1}\le\delta_k/2\}.
\]
For \(\delta\in\Delta\), let
\[
 \mathcal K_\delta:=
 \left\{F\in C(\D,\D):
   \forall k\ \forall z,w\in\D,\
   |z-w|\le\delta_k\Longrightarrow|F(z)-F(w)|\le2^{-k}
 \right\}.
\]
Fix \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), \(\delta\in\Delta\), and set
\[
 e_i(F):=\sup_{z\in\D}\dist(F(z),O_{\lambda_i}),\qquad
 \epsilon_i:=2^{-i},\qquad\beta_i:=8\,2^{-i}.
\]
For \(m\in\N\), define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal P_m(\lambda,\delta):=
 \left\{(F_0,\ldots,F_m)\in\mathcal K_\delta^{m+1}:
 \begin{gathered}
  F_0=\id_{\D},\quad
  (m\ge1\Longrightarrow F_1=\Pi|_{\D}),\\
  \forall i\in\{1,\ldots,m\},\quad F_i(\D)\subseteq K,\\
  \forall i\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\ \forall c\in O_{\lambda_i},\
                             F_i(c)=c,\\
  \forall i\in\{0,\ldots,m\},\quad e_i(F_i)\le\epsilon_i,\\
  \forall i<m,\quad\|F_{i+1}-F_i\|_\infty\le\beta_i
 \end{gathered}\right\},\label{eq:finite-certificates}\\
 \mathsf{UG}_m(\lambda,\delta):={}&
                  [\mathcal P_m(\lambda,\delta)\ne\varnothing].
 \label{eq:ug-m}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Compactness and restriction]\label{lem:compact-towers}
\(\mathcal K_\delta\) and each \(\mathcal P_m(\lambda,\delta)\)
are compact in the uniform and finite-product topologies.
Restriction from level \(m+1\) to level \(m\) is continuous and
preserves the defining conditions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The inequalities defining \(\mathcal K_\delta\) give
equicontinuity and a common compact range \(\D\).
Uniform limits preserve those inequalities and the range.
Arzel\`a--Ascoli gives compactness.
For \(F,G\in C(\D,\D)\),
\[
 |e_i(F)-e_i(G)|\le\|F-G\|_\infty.
\]
Thus fixing, range, error, and adjacent-displacement conditions
define closed subsets of the compact finite product
\(\mathcal K_\delta^{m+1}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Common finite certificates are equivalent to \(\mathsf{UG}\)]
\label{thm:hierarchy}
\[
 \mathsf{UG}\Longleftrightarrow
 \exists\lambda\in\Lambda\ \exists\delta\in\Delta\
       \forall m\in\N,\quad\mathsf{UG}_m(\lambda,\delta).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If common \(\lambda,\delta\) admit a tower at every level, let
\(X:=\mathcal K_\delta^\N\), a compact product by Tychonoff's theorem.
For each \(m\), let \(C_m\subseteq X\) consist of sequences whose
first \(m+1\) coordinates lie in \(\mathcal P_m\).
Each \(C_m\) is closed and nonempty; extend a finite tower by
repeating its last coordinate.  Also \(C_{m+1}\subseteq C_m\).
Compactness gives an element of \(\bigcap_m C_m\), which satisfies
\(\mathsf A(\lambda,F_\bullet)\).

Conversely, suppose \(\mathsf{UG}\) holds with maps \(U_n\) and
depths \(\alpha_n\).  Set \(\lambda_0=0,\lambda_1=1,j_1=0\), and,
for \(n\ge2\), define
\[
 j_n:=\max\{n+1,\ell_n,j_{n-1}+1,\lambda_{n-1}+1\},
 \qquad\lambda_n:=\alpha_{j_n}.
\]
Then \(\lambda\in\Lambda\).  Define
\[
 F_0:=\id_{\D},\qquad F_1:=\Pi|_{\D},\qquad
 F_n:=\Pi\circ U_{j_n}\quad(n\ge2).
\]
Since \(\Pi\) is nonexpansive and fixes every \(O_N\subseteq K\),
the fixing and proximity clauses hold.  For \(n\ge2\),
\[
 \|F_{n+1}-F_n\|_\infty
 \le16\,2^{-j_n}\le8\,2^{-n}.
\]
The first displacement is \(<1\); the second is at most
\(\operatorname{diam}(K)=4\).  The uniform limit theorem gives
\(U_n\to R\), and \(\Pi\circ U_{j_n}\to R\) uniformly since
\(R(\D)=\M\subseteq K\).
Uniform convergence on compact \(\D\) makes the family
\((F_n)\) equicontinuous.  Choose recursively positive scales
\(\delta_k\le2^{-k}\), \(\delta_{k+1}\le\delta_k/2\), so that
\[
 |z-w|\le\delta_k\Longrightarrow
 |F_n(z)-F_n(w)|\le2^{-k}\quad\text{for every }n.
\]
Then every finite prefix belongs to
\(\mathcal P_m(\lambda,\delta)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Every finite horizon is separately realizable]
\label{prop:finite-horizons}
\[
 \forall m\in\N\ \exists\lambda^{[m]}\in\Lambda\
 \exists\delta^{[m]}\in\Delta,\qquad
 \mathsf{UG}_m(\lambda^{[m]},\delta^{[m]}).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For \(m=0,1\), use \(\lambda\) from
Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-candidates},
\(\delta_k=2^{-k}\), and \(\id_{\D}\) and \((\id_{\D},\Pi)\).
Fix \(m\ge2\) and use \(s_m,\lambda_m\) from
Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-candidates}.
Set \(F_0=\id_{\D}\), \(F_1=\Pi|_{\D}\), and
\(F_i=Q_{s_m}\) for \(2\le i\le m\).
Set \(\lambda^{[m]}_0=0,\lambda^{[m]}_1=1,
\lambda^{[m]}_2=\lambda_m\), and
\[
 \lambda^{[m]}_i:=
 \max\{\lambda^{[m]}_{i-1}+1,\ell_i\}\quad(i\ge3).
\]
For \(2\le i\le m\), \(\lambda^{[m]}_i\ge\lambda_m\), so
\[
 O_{\lambda^{[m]}_i}\subseteq O_{\lambda_m}\subseteq E_{s_m}.
\]
Thus \(Q_{s_m}\) fixes the required outer sets, and its error is at
most \(\kappa(s_m)\le2^{-m}\le2^{-i}\).
The first displacement is \(<1\), the second is at most \(4\),
and all later displacements vanish.
The stage-zero error is at most \(2\sqrt2-2<1\); the stage-one
error is zero.  Both initial maps fix \(O_0=O_1=K\).
The three maps are uniformly continuous; a recursively decreased
common modulus supplies \(\delta^{[m]}\in\Delta\).
\end{proof}

The available quantifier order is
\[
 \forall m\in\N\ \exists\lambda^{[m]}\in\Lambda\
 \exists\delta^{[m]}\in\Delta,\quad
              \mathsf{UG}_m(\lambda^{[m]},\delta^{[m]}),
\]
whereas the hierarchy theorem requires
\[
 \exists\lambda\in\Lambda\ \exists\delta\in\Delta\
 \forall m\in\N,\quad\mathsf{UG}_m(\lambda,\delta).
\]

\subsection*{Step 11. Interpolate the tower into a continuous flow.}

\begin{theorem}[Continuous interpolation and terminal retraction]
\label{thm:interpolated-flow}
Assume \(\mathsf A(\nu,R_\bullet)\).  Put
\[
 t_n:=1-2^{-n}\qquad(n\in\N).
\]
There is a continuous map \(H:[0,1]\times\D\to\D\) such that
\begin{gather}
 H(0,z)=z,\qquad H(1,\D)=\M,\qquad
 H(t,c)=c\quad(c\in\M,\ 0\le t\le1),\label{eq:flow-endpoints}\\
 \|H(t,\cdot)-H(s,\cdot)\|_\infty\le16|t-s|,\qquad
 \|H(t,\cdot)-H(1,\cdot)\|_\infty\le16(1-t).
 \label{eq:flow-estimates}
\end{gather}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(t_n\le t\le t_{n+1}\), set
\[
 \theta:=\frac{t-t_n}{t_{n+1}-t_n},\qquad
 H(t,z):=(1-\theta)R_n(z)+\theta R_{n+1}(z).
\]
The square \(\D\) is convex.  Since
\(t_{n+1}-t_n=2^{-n-1}\), the displacement bound gives
\[
 \|\partial_tH(t,\cdot)\|_\infty
 \le\frac{8\,2^{-n}}{2^{-n-1}}=16.
\]
Thus \(H\) is \(16\)-Lipschitz in time on \([0,1)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:ug-to-mlc} supplies the uniform limit \(R\).
Set \(H(1,\cdot)=R\); the tail estimate gives
\(\|H(t,\cdot)-R\|_\infty\le16(1-t)\).
This proves continuity at \(t=1\), the endpoint conditions,
and \eqref{eq:flow-estimates}.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Step 12. Quantify each proposed map replacement.}

\begin{definition}[Relative map replacement]\label{def:map-replacement}
Let \(P\subseteq\D\), \(U\subseteq\D\) relatively open,
\(A,B\in C(\D,\D)\), and \(\chi\in C(\D,[0,1])\) satisfy
\[
 A|_P=B|_P=\id_P,\qquad\chi|_{\D\setminus U}=0.
\]
Set
\[
 S(z):=(1-\chi(z))A(z)+\chi(z)B(z),\qquad
 \alpha:=\sup_{z\in\D}\chi(z)|B(z)-A(z)|,\qquad
 d:=\|B-A\|_\infty.
\]
For \(F\in C(\D,\C)\), define
\[
 \omega_F(r):=
 \sup_{\substack{z,w\in\D\\|z-w|\le r}}|F(z)-F(w)|.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Replacement estimates]\label{lem:replacement-estimates}
The map \(S\) is continuous, maps \(\D\) into \(\D\), fixes \(P\),
and equals \(A\) on \(\D\setminus U\).  Moreover,
\begin{gather}
 \|S-A\|_\infty=\alpha\le d,\qquad
 e_i(S)\le e_i(A)+\alpha,\label{eq:replacement-error}\\
 \|S-F\|_\infty\le\|A-F\|_\infty+\alpha,\label{eq:replacement-motion}\\
 \omega_S(r)\le
 \max\{\omega_A(r),\omega_B(r)\}+d\,\omega_\chi(r).
 \label{eq:replacement-modulus}
\end{gather}
If \(A(\D),B(\D)\subseteq K\), then \(S(\D)\subseteq K\).
The maps \(A,S\) are homotopic relative to \(P\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The range, support, and fixing properties follow from the formula
and convexity of \(\D\) and \(K\).
The identity \(S-A=\chi(B-A)\) gives the first estimate;
the distance function to \(O_{\lambda_i}\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz,
giving the second.  The triangle inequality gives the third.
For \(z,w\in\D\),
\begin{align*}
 S(z)-S(w)
 ={}&(1-\chi(z))(A(z)-A(w))
     +\chi(z)(B(z)-B(w))\\
   &+(\chi(z)-\chi(w))(B(w)-A(w)).
\end{align*}
Taking absolute values proves \eqref{eq:replacement-modulus}.
The homotopy is
\[
 H(u,z):=(1-u\chi(z))A(z)+u\chi(z)B(z)
 \qquad(0\le u\le1).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Admissible one-step extension]\label{def:one-step}
Fix \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), \(\delta\in\Delta\), \(m\ge1\), and
\(\sigma=(F_0,\ldots,F_m)\in\mathcal P_m(\lambda,\delta)\).
Define
\[
 \mathcal E_m(\sigma):=
 \left\{F\in\mathcal K_\delta:
 \begin{gathered}
  F(\D)\subseteq K,\qquad
  \forall c\in O_{\lambda_{m+1}},\quad F(c)=c,\\
  e_{m+1}(F)\le2^{-(m+1)},\qquad
  \|F-F_m\|_\infty\le8\,2^{-m}
 \end{gathered}\right\}.
\]
For \(\sigma=(F_0,\ldots,F_m)\), write
\(\sigma\frown F:=(F_0,\ldots,F_m,F)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Extension-invariant criterion]\label{thm:extension-invariant}
\(\mathsf{UG}\) is equivalent to the existence of
\(\lambda\in\Lambda\), \(\delta\in\Delta\), and sets
\(\mathcal I_m\subseteq\mathcal P_m(\lambda,\delta)\) for \(m\ge1\)
such that
\begin{gather}
 (\id_{\D},\Pi|_{\D})\in\mathcal I_1,\label{eq:invariant-seed}\\
 \forall m\ge1\ \forall\sigma\in\mathcal I_m\
 \exists F\in\mathcal E_m(\sigma),\quad
                    \sigma\frown F\in\mathcal I_{m+1}.
 \label{eq:invariant-extension}
\end{gather}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equations~\eqref{eq:invariant-seed}--\eqref{eq:invariant-extension}
give recursive choices of compatible prefixes; their coordinate
sequence satisfies \(\mathsf A(\lambda,F_\bullet)\).
Conversely, the common-data hierarchy in
Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy} gives a normalized infinite tower.
Let \(\mathcal I_m\) consist of its single prefix through level \(m\).
The next coordinate verifies \eqref{eq:invariant-extension}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative repair after an admissible extension]
\label{thm:repair-equivalence}
For \(\sigma\in\mathcal P_m\), let
\[
 \mathcal H_{m+1}:=
 \{F\in\mathcal K_\delta:F(\D)\subseteq K,\
                    F|_{O_{\lambda_{m+1}}}=\id\},
\]
\[
 J_m(F;\sigma):=
 \max\left\{
 \frac{e_{m+1}(F)}{2^{-(m+1)}},
 \frac{\|F-F_m\|_\infty}{8\,2^{-m}}
 \right\}.
\]
The minimum \(q_m(\sigma):=\min_{F\in\mathcal H_{m+1}}J_m(F;\sigma)\)
is attained, and \(q_m(\sigma)\le1\) if and only if
\(\mathcal E_m(\sigma)\ne\varnothing\).
If \(F\in\mathcal E_m(\sigma)\), then
\[
 A_k:=2^{-k}F_m+(1-2^{-k})F\qquad(k\in\N)
\]
converges uniformly to \(F\) and satisfies
\begin{gather}
 A_0=F_m,\qquad A_k\in\mathcal H_{m+1},\label{eq:repair-class}\\
 \|A_{k+1}-A_k\|_\infty\le8\,2^{-m-k-1},\label{eq:repair-motion}\\
 e_{m+1}(A_k)\le2^{-(m+1)}+8\,2^{-m-k}.
 \label{eq:repair-error}
\end{gather}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\(\mathcal H_{m+1}\) is nonempty because \(\Pi|_{\D}\in\mathcal H_{m+1}\).
It is closed in compact \(\mathcal K_\delta\).
The error and uniform-distance functionals are continuous, so \(J_m\)
attains its minimum.  The equivalence \(q_m(\sigma)\le1\) iff
\(\mathcal E_m(\sigma)\ne\varnothing\) is the definition of \(J_m\).
Since \(\lambda_{m+1}>\lambda_m\), \(F_m\) fixes
\(O_{\lambda_{m+1}}\).  Constant convex combinations preserve the
common modulus, the range \(K\), and fixing.
The inequalities follow from
\[
 \|A_{k+1}-A_k\|_\infty
 =2^{-k-1}\|F-F_m\|_\infty
\]
and the \(1\)-Lipschitz property of distance to
\(O_{\lambda_{m+1}}\).
\end{proof}

\noindent
For a chosen \(F\in\mathcal E_m(\sigma)\), the sequence \(A_k\)
converges to \(F\) while retaining the range, modulus, and fixed-set
constraints and satisfying explicit displacement and error bounds.

\subsection*{Step 13. State the remaining proof obligations.}

For the explicit heights of Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-candidates},
the remaining first-path assertion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remaining-motion}
 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
       \|Q_{s_{n+1}}-Q_{s_n}\|_\infty<\infty.
\end{equation}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:summable-cutoff}, proving
\eqref{eq:remaining-motion} would prove \(\LC(\M)\).
The rate-free categorical target is compactness of the cutoff family
in Theorem~\ref{thm:categorical-cutoff-compactness}; that target is
itself equivalent to MLC.  Summable movement is one sufficient
certificate for it.
The finite-band estimate \eqref{eq:candidate-partial-motion} reduces
the stronger sufficient condition
\[
 \int_0^aV(u)\,du<\infty
\]
to the required summability.  The alternate finite-tower route
requires one common pair \((\lambda,\delta)\) and the
invariant-preserving extension \eqref{eq:invariant-extension} at
every level.  The categorical structures are established; their
compactness or a common compatible extension remains to be proved.

\begin{summarytheorem}[Strongest unconditional theorem proved]
There are \(\lambda\in\N^\N\) and \(T_\bullet\in C(\D,\D)^\N\)
satisfying \eqref{eq:opening-depths}--\eqref{eq:opening-image-gap}.
\end{summarytheorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:exterior-candidates} constructs these sequences and
establishes each assertion.
\end{proof}
