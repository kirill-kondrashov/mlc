\section{Polynomial-like maps}

\begin{definition}[Polynomial-like map]
\label{def:polynomial-like}
A polynomial-like map of degree $d$ is a proper holomorphic map
\[
 g:U\longrightarrow V
\]
where $U,V\subset\C$ are topological disks, $U\Subset V$, and
$d\ge2$.  Properness means that the inverse image of each compact
subset of $V$ is compact in $U$.  The degree is the number of
preimages of a regular value, counted with multiplicity.
Its filled Julia set is
\[
 K(g):=\{z\in U:g^{\circ n}(z)\in U\text{ for every }n\in\N\},
\]
 where an iterate is required to remain in the domain at each preceding
 step.
 \end{definition}

 The compact-containment condition is essential.  A proper map
 $\C\to\C$ is not, with those domains, a polynomial-like restriction:
 its source closure is not compactly contained in its target.
 The current construction \lean{parameterToBMol_spec} uses
 $U=V=\C$ to obtain a molecule-model object.  That typed object does
 not by itself supply the domains in Definition~\ref{def:polynomial-like}.

 \section{Hybrid equivalence and straightening}

 \begin{definition}[Quasiconformal map]
 An orientation-preserving homeomorphism $h$ between plane domains is
 quasiconformal if it has locally square-integrable weak first
 derivatives and
 \[
  |\overline\partial h|\le k|\partial h|\quad\text{almost everywhere}
  \qquad\text{for some }0\le k<1.
 \]
 Its maximal dilatation is at most $(1+k)/(1-k)$.
 \end{definition}

 \begin{definition}[Hybrid equivalence]
 Polynomial-like maps $g_1,g_2$ are hybrid equivalent if a
 quasiconformal conjugacy $h$ is defined on neighbourhoods of their
 filled Julia sets and satisfies
 \[
  h\circ g_1=g_2\circ h,\qquad
  \overline\partial h=0\quad\text{almost everywhere on }K(g_1).
 \]
 \end{definition}

 \begin{theorem}[Douady--Hubbard straightening]
 Every degree-$d$ polynomial-like map is hybrid equivalent to a
 degree-$d$ polynomial.  If its filled Julia set is connected, the
 polynomial is unique up to affine conjugacy.  In the connected
 degree-two case, monic centred normalization gives a unique
 polynomial $z^2+c$.
 \end{theorem}

 The theorem is a classical analytic input~\cite{DHPolynomialLike}.
 Its proof constructs an invariant Beltrami structure outside the
 filled Julia set and integrates it by the measurable Riemann mapping
 theorem.  Normalization and rigidity produce uniqueness.  Formalizing
 that argument requires more than a record containing a chosen
 straightening parameter: the conjugacy, its domain, and the vanishing
 Beltrami coefficient are part of the proof.

 \section{Renormalization periods and little Julia sets}

 \begin{definition}[Quadratic renormalization]
\label{def:quadratic-renormalization}
A quadratic polynomial $f_c$ is renormalizable with period $p$ if there are
domains $U\Subset V$ containing the critical point and an integer $p>1$ such
that
\[
 f_c^{\circ p}:U\to V
\]
is polynomial-like of degree two with connected filled Julia set and
the prescribed little-Julia-set orbit combinatorics.  The
renormalization is the normalized restriction with its affine and
external marking.  The orbit of the little filled Julia set
$K'=K(f_c^{\circ p}|_U)$ consists of
$K',f_c(K'),\ldots,f_c^{\circ(p-1)}(K')$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Renormalization tower]
\label{def:renormalization-tower}
A marked renormalization tower is a sequence
\[
 g_0,\ g_1,\ g_2,\ldots
\]
of marked polynomial-like maps together with renormalization relations
$g_n\rightsquigarrow g_{n+1}$, where $g_{n+1}$ is a normalized
restriction of $g_n^{\circ p_n}$.  The return period measured in the
original dynamics is
\[
 T_0=1,\qquad T_{n+1}=T_np_n.
\]
where $p_n$ is the period of the $n$-th renormalization step.
\end{definition}

The Lean structure \lean{RenormalizationTower} stores a sequence of
molecule models and a nonempty relation at each step.  Its existing
\lean{cumulativePeriod} is an additive indexing sequence.  The
monotonicity theorem for that sequence is valid arithmetic, but its
value is not $T_n$ above.  The review's separate definition
\lean{relativePeriodProduct} records the multiplicative bookkeeping;
Chapter~\ref{chap:combinatorics} gives the exact comparison.

\section{Principal nests and modulus}

\begin{definition}[Principal nest]
\label{def:principal-nest}
For a quadratic map with an appropriate puzzle, a principal nest is a
nested sequence
\[
 V_0\supset V_1\supset V_2\supset\cdots
\]
of critical puzzle domains selected by successive critical first
returns.  The return time is the least positive iterate bringing the
critical point back to the selected domain, when that return exists.
The associated first-return map can have several branches; its
critical branch determines the next critical domain.
The principal-nest annuli, at levels with proper nesting, are
\[
 A_n^{\mathrm{pn}}:=V_n\setminus\overline{V_{n+1}}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[A priori bounds]
\label{def:apriori-bounds}
A given tower has a priori bounds if there exists $\mu>0$ such that
\[
 \modulus(A_n^{\mathrm{pn}})\ge\mu
\qquad\text{for every specified nondegenerate level }n.
\]
For polynomial-like renormalizations the analogous bound is
$\modulus(V_n\setminus\overline{U_n})\ge\mu$.
Bounds uniform over a family require the same $\mu$ for all its
towers.  Beau bounds require a family-uniform eventual lower bound,
while the entry level may depend on the initial geometry.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform lower bounds imply divergence]
\label{lem:uniform-bound-divergence}
If $\modulus(A_n^{\mathrm{pn}})\ge\mu>0$ for every $n$, then
\[
 \sum_n\modulus(A_n^{\mathrm{pn}})=+\infty.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The partial sums dominate $N\mu$ and therefore are unbounded.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Shrinking of a bounded principal nest]
\label{thm:renormalization-to-puzzle}
Suppose the principal domains are bounded Jordan domains containing
$0$, their closures are properly nested, and every intervening annulus
has conformal modulus at least $\mu>0$.  Then
$\bigcap_n\overline{V_n}=\{0\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The modulus series diverges by Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-bound-divergence}.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:yoccoz-shrink} to this nest.
\end{proof}

This theorem concerns conformal moduli of genuine annuli.  The current
Lean declaration
\lean{PrincipalNestTarget.paraPuzzle_shrink_of_uniformConformalLowerBoundTarget}
instead uses Gaussian areas of frozen differences.
Corollary~\ref{cor:no-frozen-certificate} proves that its lower-bound
input is impossible.  It therefore cannot serve as the formal
implementation of the classical theorem above.

Likewise, serial annuli satisfy the Grötzsch inequality, generally not
an arbitrary finite-additivity identity.  Passing between principal
and ordinary puzzle scales requires the actual analytic comparison
from the source; a set partition by itself supplies no conformal
modulus formula.

\section{Parameter-plane conclusion}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional parameter endpoint]
\label{thm:satellite-apriori-mlc}
Let $c\in\M$.  Suppose a renormalization argument and its
phase--parameter construction produce open nested parameter pieces
$Q_n$ containing $c$, with compact closures, connected intersections
$Q_n\cap\M$, and
$\bigcap_n\overline{Q_n}=\{c\}$.
Then $\M$ is locally connected at $c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:parameter-shrinking}.  This model-independent
step is implemented by
\leanref{Mlc/Literature/Foundations.lean}{localConnectivity_of_compact_parameterPieces}.
The analytic argument must supply its hypotheses in parameter space;
dynamical shrinking alone has not yet supplied them.
\end{proof}

\section{Primitive, satellite, and neutral regimes}

The renormalization type is not cosmetic:
\begin{itemize}
\item primitive renormalization produces pairwise disjoint little filled Julia sets and
      is the natural setting for principal-nest modulus estimates;
\item satellite renormalization has little filled Julia sets meeting at
      marked periodic points and requires control of rotation data;
\item neutral renormalization introduces Siegel disks, bubbles, hedgehogs,
      and neutral cascades, where ordinary expanding estimates degenerate.
\end{itemize}

Lyubich's bounded-type and a priori-bound machinery, Dudko--Lyubich's
satellite and Feigenbaum results, and the 2025--2026 Kahn/Dudko/Lim papers
motivate a common proof architecture~\cite{LyubichDynamics,LyubichIII}.
Their analytic estimates differ; a source-specific formalization must
construct genuine marked towers, prove conformal control, realize
connected parameter pieces, and invoke the topological transport
theorem.  This requires replacing the refuted frozen certificate,
rather than filling its fields.

\begin{formalobligation}[Renormalization existence and estimates]
\label{est:renormalization}
For each literature class, define the exact combinatorial predicate,
construct the corresponding marked tower, formalize the relevant a priori
bounds, and prove that its parameter parapuzzles satisfy the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:satellite-apriori-mlc}.
\end{formalobligation}
