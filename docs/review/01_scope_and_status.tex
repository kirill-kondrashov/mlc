\label{chap:scope}

\section{The target and the two planes}

Let
\[
 f_c(z)=z^2+c,\qquad p_0(c)=0,\qquad p_{n+1}(c)=f_c(p_n(c)),
\]
and let
\[
 \M=\{c\in\C:\sup_n |p_n(c)|<\infty\}
\]
be the Mandelbrot set.  The global MLC conjecture is
\[
 \mathsf{MLC}:\qquad \LocallyConnectedSpace(\M).
\]
The pointwise statement asks that, for a specified $c\in\M$, every
neighbourhood of $c$ in $\M$ contain a connected neighbourhood of $c$.
The global statement quantifies over all such parameters.
Chapter~\ref{chap:quadratic-foundations} develops the orbit definitions
and escape criterion; Chapter~\ref{chap:topological-foundations} gives
the precise neighbourhood formulation and its proved endpoint lemmas.

The first distinction of this review is therefore:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a theorem about a parameter class;
\item a theorem about local connectivity of the corresponding Julia set;
\item a theorem about local connectivity of the Mandelbrot set at those
      parameters; and
\item the global statement $\mathsf{MLC}$.
\end{enumerate}
These statements are related by the phase--parameter machinery, but they are
not interchangeable.  The Lean declarations
\lean{Mlc.Literature.MandelbrotLocallyConnectedAt} and
\lean{Mlc.Literature.GlobalMLC} keep this distinction explicit.

\section{What a theorem-coverage picture can show}

\input{figures/mlc_status_schematic.tex}

Figure~\ref{fig:mlc-status} gives the global geometric context.
The Yoccoz mechanism, the infinite-renormalization cases, and the
unresolved-coverage question have separate figures in their respective
chapters.  This separation keeps the theorem hypotheses and the visual
evidence on the same page.

Open subsets of the plane are locally connected, so interior parameters
have an elementary local argument.  The boundary classes are instead
described by dynamical or combinatorial conditions.  A ``bounded-type''
class is not an entire coloured bulb, and a Feigenbaum class is not
identified by the apparent texture of a rendered pixel.

\begin{definition}[Coverage relative to a theorem list]
Let $\mathscr C_j\subseteq\partial\M$ be the parameter classes in a
specified list of published pointwise MLC theorems, with all of their
source hypotheses retained.  Define
\[
 \mathscr K=\bigcup_j\mathscr C_j,\qquad
 \mathscr U_{\rm cov}=\partial\M\setminus\mathscr K.
\]
\end{definition}

This is a set-theoretic record of what that list asserts.  Membership in
$\mathscr U_{\rm cov}$ means only that the list provides no applicable
theorem.  It neither asserts failure of local connectivity nor rules out
coverage by another theorem.  Computing this complement at every pixel
would require an exact parameter-classification procedure that the
renderer does not provide.

\begin{conditionalstep}
There is no established set of counterexamples to MLC to colour in the
picture.  The unresolved illustration displays numerical small-scale
geometry alongside the remaining analytic obligations.  Its colour
denotes finite-time orbit behaviour, while its caption describes the
proof frontier.
\end{conditionalstep}

\section{What remains open}

The full conjecture remains open.  The main unresolved frontier is the
parameter-plane control needed for general infinitely renormalizable
quadratics, in particular cases with unbounded combinatorics.  Recent
bounded-type satellite and Feigenbaum results enlarge the proved set of
parameters, but do not supply a uniform theorem for every boundary
parameter.

For a covered literature class, the remaining task may be formalization
of an already published analytic argument.  For unrestricted parameters,
the missing analytic argument can also be an open mathematical problem.
These are different kinds of incompleteness.

The current Lean root proves the implication
\[
 \mathrm{BUF}(\M,O)\Longrightarrow\mathsf{MLC},
\]
with the buffer retained as a hypothesis.  Generic graph-cut certificate
interfaces and topological transport theorems are also available.
Chapter~\ref{chap:frozen-audit} explains why the inherited
Gaussian-area puzzle model contributes no instances of its purported
finite-renormalization class.  That obstruction reduces the claimed
formal coverage, while leaving the independently proved topological
implications intact.
