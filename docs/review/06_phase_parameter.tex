\section{External-ray data}

The normalized Böttcher coordinate gives a potential--angle
parametrization of the basin of infinity.  In the formalization we separate
the analytic parametrization from its boundary behavior.

\begin{definition}[External-ray system]
\label{def:external-ray-system}
For a quadratic map $f_c$, an external-ray system consists of maps
\[
 \gamma_{c,\theta}:(0,\infty)\to\C,\qquad
  \ell_c(\theta)\in\C,\qquad\theta\in\Theta\subseteq\R/\Z,
\]
such that
\[
 \lim_{t\to+\infty}\gamma_{c,\theta}(t)=\infty,\qquad
 \lim_{t\to0^+}\gamma_{c,\theta}(t)=\ell_c(\theta)
\]
whenever the ray lands.  The dynamical relation is
\[
 f_c(\gamma_{c,\theta}(t))
 =\gamma_{c,2\theta}(2t).
\]
The angles in $\Theta$ are the selected landing angles; the construction
does not assume that every external ray lands.  In the potential
parametrization one additionally has
$G_c(\gamma_{c,\theta}(t))=t$ for $t>0$.  A finite puzzle graph uses
only finitely many certified landing angles and their required pullbacks.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The current Lean structure \lean{ExternalRaySystem} is a preliminary
interface with a different literal specification: it records a total
landing map, a limit at \lean{atTop}, continuity, angle periodicity,
and the doubling identity.  It has no potential or escape field.
Thus it does not yet encode the positive-potential definition above.
In particular, its name cannot be used as evidence that landing rays
have been constructed.  A source realization must replace or strengthen
this preliminary specification, including the correct boundary filter.
\end{remark}

\section{Holomorphic motion and the critical-value diagonal}

\begin{definition}[Holomorphic motion]
Let $\Lambda\subseteq\C$ be a parameter domain with base point
$c_0$, and let $E\subseteq\C$.  A holomorphic motion of $E$ over
$\Lambda$ is a map $h:\Lambda\times E\to\C$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $h(c_0,z)=z$ for every $z\in E$;
\item for fixed $z$, the map $c\mapsto h(c,z)$ is holomorphic;
\item for fixed $c$, the map $z\mapsto h(c,z)$ is injective.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Repelling periodic points move holomorphically as long as their
multipliers avoid $1$.  Appropriate landing rays and equipotential
markings then move over a parameter domain where their combinatorics
persist.  The $\lambda$-lemma extends a holomorphic motion to its
closure, with quasiconformal control after the relevant extensions.
This is the geometric mechanism behind the marked phase--parameter
comparison~\cite{LyubichBook,LyubichIII}.

The critical value in the quadratic family is the point $c$ itself.
Consequently one studies the diagonal relation
\[
 c=h(c,z),
\]
where $z$ ranges over a marked base dynamical piece.  Solving this
relation and controlling its parameter domains is different from
translating the base piece by $c$.  Properness, degree, boundary
markings, and distortion estimates are needed to identify the resulting
parapuzzle and compare its geometry with the dynamical puzzle.

\section{Dynamical puzzle graphs}

\begin{definition}[Finite dynamical graph]
\label{def:finite-dynamical-graph}
Choose finitely many angles whose rays land and add the equipotential arcs
of a fixed positive potential.  Pulling this finite graph back under
$f_c^{\circ n}$ produces a graph $\Gamma_n(c)$.  The critical puzzle piece
is the component
\[
 P_n(c):=\operatorname{Comp}_0(\C\setminus\Gamma_n(c)).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Puzzle properties]
\label{prop:puzzle-properties}
The required finite-level data are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Gamma_n(c)$ is closed and $P_n(c)$ is open;
\item $0\in P_n(c)$ and $P_n(c)$ is connected;
\item $P_{n+1}(c)\subseteq P_n(c)$;
\item the annulus $P_n(c)\setminus\overline{P_{n+1}(c)}$ carries a
      conformal modulus.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The first three properties are topological consequences or required
features of a suitable graph construction.  The fourth is analytic and
requires proper nesting and control of critical degeneracies.
In the current Lean structure \lean{DynamicalPuzzleSystem}, closed
graphs and open connected nested pieces are fields, but the equality
between a piece and a component of the graph complement is not a field.
Nor is there a conformal annulus-modulus field.  The structure is
therefore preliminary data, rather than a construction of the four
classical properties.

\section{Parameter graphs}

\begin{definition}[Phase--parameter realization]
\label{def:phase-parameter-realization}
A phase--parameter realization at $c\in\M$ consists of parameter graphs
$\mathcal G_n$ and parameter pieces $\mathcal P_n(c)$ such that:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathcal G_n\text{ is closed},&
\mathcal P_n(c)\text{ is open},\\
\mathcal P_{n+1}(c)\subseteq\mathcal P_n(c),&
c\in\mathcal P_n(c),\\
\mathcal P_n(c)\cap\M\text{ is connected},&
\bigcap_n\overline{\mathcal P_n(c)}=\{c\}.
\end{array}
\]
The closures are compact.  The phase--parameter compatibility
additionally identifies the finite marked combinatorial data of
$\mathcal P_n(c)$ with those of $P_n(c)$ through the moving graphs.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Topological transport]
\label{thm:topological-transport}
The parameter pieces in
Definition~\ref{def:phase-parameter-realization} imply local
connectedness of $\M$ at $c$.  The same conclusion holds if their
neighbourhood-basis property is supplied directly instead of derived
from compact closure shrinking.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the compact closure version, apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:parameter-shrinking}.  In the basis version, the
connected sets $\mathcal P_n(c)\cap\M$ are neighbourhoods of $c$ in
the subspace and refine every neighbourhood there.  This is precisely
the connected-basis criterion.
\end{proof}

The code
\leanref{Mlc/Literature/PhaseParameter.lean}{localConnectivity_of_phaseParameterData}
proves the basis version: the structure supplies the field
\lean{parameter_basis} explicitly.
Its additional \lean{phase_parameter_compatibility} field is only a
metric proximity condition to translated dynamical pieces.  That field
is unused in the topological proof and does not encode holomorphic
motion, graph combinatorics, or properness.  Those analytic properties
must be formalized separately before interpreting a constructor as the
classical phase--parameter theorem.

\begin{conditionalstep}
The difficult theorem is not Theorem~\ref{thm:topological-transport}, but
the existence of the data in Definition~\ref{def:phase-parameter-realization}.
The classical construction combines selected external-ray landing,
graph pullbacks, holomorphic motion, properness, and identification of
the parameter image.  The proposition
\lean{YoccozPhaseParameterResult} currently asks for the preliminary
Lean data at every parameter of $\M$.  Its topological conclusion is
checked, but its name does not assert that the classical construction
or the equivalence of the specifications has been proved.
\end{conditionalstep}

\section{The full Green-sublevel warning}

For $c\in\M$ one can form the larger sets
\[
 \widetilde P_n(c):=
 \{c'\in\C:G_c(c'-c)<2^{-n}\}.
\]
They are useful analytic neighbourhoods, but they are not automatically
the graph-cut parapuzzle pieces of Definition~\ref{def:phase-parameter-realization}.
In fact, the full-sublevel intersection can retain a translate of the
filled Julia set.  Thus the implication
\[
 \widetilde P_n(c)\text{ connected}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal P_n(c)\text{ is a valid parapuzzle}
\]
is invalid without a separate carving or quotient theorem.

\begin{formalobligation}[Phase--parameter realization in a source class]
\label{est:phase-parameter}
Define the finite selected landing-ray data with the physical potential
parameter, construct the graph-cut puzzles and their marked motions, and
prove that the resulting parameter pieces are connected in $\M$,
nested, and shrinking in the compact closure sense.  For a published
MLC class this is a formalization task; a universal construction
covering unrestricted parameters would additionally resolve an open
mathematical problem.
\end{formalobligation}
