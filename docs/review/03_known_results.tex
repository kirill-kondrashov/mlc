\section{Classical classes}

The classical route combines hyperbolic or subhyperbolic dynamics, puzzle
pieces, and the phase--parameter correspondence.  The main classes to record
are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Interior parameters.}  Local connectivity is local in an
      open subset of the plane.
\item \textbf{Hyperbolic and subhyperbolic cases.}  Expansion or
      attracting-cycle control gives finite geometric models and parameter
      control; the foundational source is Douady--Hubbard.
\item \textbf{Misiurewicz and related postcritically finite cases.}
      Finite orbit combinatorics and landing data, treated in the
      Douady--Hubbard/Yoccoz framework.
\item \textbf{Finitely renormalizable cases.}  Divergent puzzle moduli and
      shrinking parameter pieces, with Yoccoz as the primary source.
\end{itemize}

The exact hypotheses matter.  The review will not use the informal phrase
``all regular parameters'' as a Lean theorem name; each class receives a
predicate and a proof obligation.  The classical puzzle conclusions are
recorded in Hubbard's exposition of Yoccoz's
theorems~\cite{HubbardYoccoz}; Dudko's
survey~\cite[Section~2]{DudkoMLC} explains their reduction of the
remaining global problem to nested renormalization copies.
The legacy code predicate \lean{FinitelyRenormalizable} has a different,
empty meaning, as proved in Chapter~\ref{chap:frozen-audit}.

\begin{definition}[Finite renormalization depth]
A quadratic polynomial is finitely renormalizable when the lengths
of its successive nontrivial marked quadratic-like
renormalization chains are bounded.  A nonrenormalizable polynomial
has depth zero.  Each step has relative period at least two and uses
the genuine little-Julia-set orbit combinatorics of
Definition~\ref{def:quadratic-renormalization}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Yoccoz: the repelling finite-depth case]
\label{thm:classical-yoccoz-finite}
Let $c\in\M$.  Suppose every periodic cycle of $f_c$ is repelling
and $f_c$ is finitely renormalizable.  Then $J_c$ is locally
connected and $\M$ is locally connected at $c$.
\end{theorem}

This is a classical source theorem~\cite{HubbardYoccoz,DudkoMLC},
whose proof uses graph-cut puzzles, their geometric shrinking, and
the separate parameter-plane construction.  The two conclusions
concern different spaces; one is not obtained by merely translating
the other.  Parabolic parameters and attracting interiors belong
to the additional classical cases above.  The source theorem
retains its stated hypotheses, rather than asserting the same
proof for a Cremer or Siegel cycle.

The theorem is included here with its mathematical definitions.
Its complete analytic Lean proof remains a formalization
obligation; the empty legacy predicate supplies no substitute.

\section{Lyubich's machinery}

Lyubich's work supplies the renormalization and geometric language needed
after the finitely renormalizable case: principal nests, modulus estimates,
rigidity, a priori bounds, and parapuzzles.  The 1997 Acta Mathematica
papers are the core reference for the renormalization framework.  The
Astérisque paper
\emph{Dynamics of quadratic polynomials, III: parapuzzle and SBR measures}
is kept as a separate research article in the inventory.

The online book \emph{Conformal Geometry and Dynamics of Quadratic
Polynomials} is listed by its author as volumes I--II.
The repository's 2013 ICTP notes are another version of that expository
project.  Neither the Astérisque article's ``III'' nor the ``IV'' in
Kahn--Lyubich's a priori-bound series establishes an additional volume
of the book.  These sources have separate bibliographic
entries~\cite{LyubichBook,LyubichNotes,LyubichIII,KahnLyubichElephant}.

\section{Dudko--Lyubich results}

Two recent parameter classes are especially important for this project:
\begin{enumerate}
\item bounded-type satellite parameters, treated in the GAFA paper and
      \lean{arXiv:1808.10425};
\item all infinitely renormalizable parameters of bounded combinatorics,
      called Feigenbaum points in arXiv:2309.02107 and the 2026
      Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS publication.
\end{enumerate}
Both results use substantial renormalization, a priori-bound, and
parameter-puzzle machinery.  The formalization target is not merely the
final sentence ``MLC holds at $c$'', but the chain of definitions and lemmas
that produces the shrinking connected parameter basis.

\section{The remaining nested-copy problem}

A small Mandelbrot copy is a quadratic-like renormalization locus
equipped with its marked straightening homeomorphism onto $\M$.
The corresponding tuning map embeds $\M$ back into that locus.
An infinite strictly nested sequence of such copies records
successive renormalizations:
\[
 \M_1\supsetneq\M_2\supsetneq\cdots.
\]
Each copy is compact and connected.  These two properties guarantee a
nonempty connected intersection, but do not guarantee a singleton.
Dudko records the Yoccoz reduction in the
form~\cite[Section~2, equation~(2.1)]{DudkoMLC}
\[
 \text{every such nested renormalization chain has singleton intersection}.
\]
Proving this remaining rigidity statement in full generality would close
the global problem through the classical reduction.

The bounded-combinatorics theorem handles an important family of such
chains.  The 2026 primitive parabolic-tail theorem handles a further
class whose periods can be unbounded.  Therefore ``unbounded'' by
itself does not mean ``unproved''.  The frontier includes the
unrestricted satellite problem and the interpolation between
renormalization regimes described in
\cite[Section~4]{DudkoMLC}.  The exact union of the named covered
classes must retain each theorem's additional hypotheses.
