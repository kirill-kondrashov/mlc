# Proof of the remaining monodromy problem at $$c=2$$

Let
$$
V=\{z\in\mathbb C:\lvert z\rvert>4\},\qquad \Omega=\{w\in\mathbb C:\lvert w\rvert>1\},
$$
and let
$$
\phi:V\to\Omega
$$
be the restricted outside Böttcher map for $$f(z)=z^2+2$$.

Assume:

1. $$\phi$$ is a local homeomorphism.
2. There exists an integer $$d\ge 1$$ such that, for every $$w\in\Omega$$, the fiber $$\phi^{-1}(w)$$ has exactly $$d$$ points.
3. For some $$R>4$$, the loop
   $$
   \gamma_R:S^1\to\Omega,\qquad \gamma_R(e^{2\pi i t})=\phi(Re^{2\pi i t}),
   $$
   is freely homotopic in $$\Omega$$ to the positively oriented circle
   $$
   \sigma_R:S^1\to\Omega,\qquad \sigma_R(e^{2\pi i t})=Re^{2\pi i t}.
   $$

We prove that $$d=1$$.

## Proof

Because every fiber has exactly $$d\ge 1$$ points, the map $$\phi$$ is surjective. Since $$\phi$$ is a local homeomorphism and every fiber is finite of the same cardinality, $$\phi:V\to\Omega$$ is a finite-sheeted covering map of degree $$d$$. In particular, for any base point $$v_0\in V$$, the subgroup
$$
\phi_*\bigl(\pi_1(V,v_0)\bigr)\subset \pi_1(\Omega,\phi(v_0))
$$
has index $$d$$.

Now both $$V$$ and $$\Omega$$ are annuli:
$$
V=\mathbb C\setminus \overline{D(0,4)},\qquad \Omega=\mathbb C\setminus \overline{D(0,1)}.
$$
Hence
$$
\pi_1(V)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad \pi_1(\Omega)\cong\mathbb Z.
$$
For the chosen radius $$R>4$$, the circle
$$
c_R:S^1\to V,\qquad c_R(e^{2\pi i t})=Re^{2\pi i t},
$$
is a positive generator of $$\pi_1(V)$$. Therefore the subgroup
$$
\phi_*\bigl(\pi_1(V)\bigr)\subset \pi_1(\Omega)\cong\mathbb Z
$$
is generated by
$$
\phi_*([c_R])=[\gamma_R].
$$
Since this subgroup has index $$d$$, it must be the subgroup $$d\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Z$$. Equivalently, under the winding-number identification of $$\pi_1(\Omega)$$ with $$\mathbb Z$$, the class $$[\gamma_R]$$ has absolute value $$d$$:
$$
[\gamma_R]=\pm d.
$$

On the other hand, assumption 3 says that $$\gamma_R$$ is freely homotopic in $$\Omega$$ to $$\sigma_R$$. Free homotopy classes of loops in an annulus are classified by winding number about the origin, and the positively oriented circle $$\sigma_R$$ has winding number $$1$$. Hence
$$
[\gamma_R]=[\sigma_R]=1
$$
under the same identification. Therefore
$$
\pm d=1.
$$
Since $$d\ge 1$$ is an integer, it follows that
$$
d=1.
$$

This proves the statement.
