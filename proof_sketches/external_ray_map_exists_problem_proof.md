# Proof of the remaining monodromy problem at $$c=2$$

Let
$$
V=\{z\in\mathbb C:\lvert z\rvert>4\},\qquad \Omega=\{w\in\mathbb C:\lvert w\rvert>1\},
$$
and let
$$
\phi:V\to\Omega
$$
be the restricted outside Böttcher map at $$c=2$$.

Assume:

1. $$\phi$$ is a local homeomorphism.
2. There is an integer $$d\ge 1$$ such that every fiber $$\phi^{-1}(w)$$ has exactly $$d$$ points.
3. For every sufficiently large radius $$R>4$$, the loop
   $$
   \gamma_R(t)=\phi(Re^{2\pi i t}),\qquad t\in[0,1],
   $$
   is freely homotopic in $$\Omega$$ to the positively oriented unit-circle generator
   $$
   \sigma(t)=e^{2\pi i t}.
   $$

We prove that $$d=1$$.

## Proof

Since $$V$$ is connected and locally path connected and $$\phi:V\to\Omega$$ is a local homeomorphism with constant finite fiber cardinality $$d$$, the map $$\phi$$ is a covering map of degree $$d$$. Equivalently, for every $$w\in\Omega$$, the fiber $$\phi^{-1}(w)$$ has exactly $$d$$ points, so the subgroup
$$
\phi_*\bigl(\pi_1(V,v_0)\bigr)\subset \pi_1(\Omega,\phi(v_0))
$$
has index $$d$$ for every base point $$v_0\in V$$.

Now $$V=\mathbb C\setminus \overline{D(0,4)}$$ and $$\Omega=\mathbb C\setminus \overline{D(0,1)}$$ are annuli, hence
$$
\pi_1(V)\cong \mathbb Z,\qquad \pi_1(\Omega)\cong \mathbb Z.
$$
For every $$R>4$$, the circle
$$
c_R(t)=Re^{2\pi i t}
$$
is a positive generator of $$\pi_1(V)$$. Therefore the induced subgroup
$$
\phi_*\bigl(\pi_1(V)\bigr)\subset \pi_1(\Omega)\cong\mathbb Z
$$
is generated by $$\phi_*([c_R])=[\gamma_R]$$ and has index $$d$$. The only index-$$d$$ subgroups of $$\mathbb Z$$ are $$d\mathbb Z$$, so under the identification $$\pi_1(\Omega)\cong\mathbb Z$$ by winding number we obtain
$$
[\gamma_R]=\pm d.
$$

On the other hand, assumption 3 says that $$\gamma_R$$ is freely homotopic in $$\Omega$$ to $$\sigma$$. In an annulus, free homotopy classes are classified by winding number, so
$$
[\gamma_R]=[\sigma]=1
$$
up to the same winding-number identification. Hence
$$
\pm d=1.
$$
Because $$d\ge 1$$ is an integer, this forces
$$
d=1.
$$

This proves the claim.
